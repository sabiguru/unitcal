ISSUE 1 — BROKEN BREADCRUMB URL (Code Bug)
Priority: CRITICAL — Fix Immediately
----------------------------------------------------------------

WHAT IS WRONG:
Your JSON-LD breadcrumb structured data contains a JavaScript bug
on every single page. The third breadcrumb item resolves to:

  "item": "https://calcfort.comundefined"

The word "undefined" is literally being concatenated into the URL
because a variable holding the current page path is not being
passed correctly to the breadcrumb component. This affects every
page on the site.

Google's structured data parser reads this, flags it as an error,
and treats the entire page's structured data as untrustworthy.
This is a site-wide trust signal problem.

HOW TO FIX:
In your Next.js breadcrumb component, find where the third
breadcrumb item URL is being constructed. It will look something
like:

  item: "https://calcfort.com" + slug  (where slug is undefined)

Fix it so the full canonical URL is passed correctly. Example of
what the correct output should look like:

  {
    "@type": "ListItem",
    "position": 3,
    "name": "Percentage Calculator",
    "item": "https://calcfort.com/calculator/mathematics/percentage"
  }

The "item" field for the current page should use the page's own
canonical URL, which you already have available in your page
metadata. Pass it through to the breadcrumb component properly.

After fixing, validate every page type using:
  https://search.google.com/test/rich-results

EXPECTED OUTCOME:
Structured data errors resolved across all 222 pages. Google
regains trust in your schema markup.

ISSUE 2 — OG TAGS POINTING TO HOMEPAGE ON EVERY PAGE
Priority: CRITICAL — Fix Immediately
----------------------------------------------------------------

WHAT IS WRONG:
Every single calculator and learn page on your site currently
sends Google these identical Open Graph tags:

  <meta property="og:url" content="https://calcfort.com" />
  <meta property="og:title" content="Calcfort — Free Online Calculators" />
  <meta property="og:description" content="Free calculators for maths,
    finance, health and engineering. Get instant results..." />

When Google crawls 222 pages and they all declare og:url as the
homepage, Google treats them all as duplicates of each other and
of the homepage. This is directly why "Google-selected canonical"
was BLANK in Search Console — Google could not distinguish any
of your pages from one another.

This is the primary reason none of your pages are being indexed.

HOW TO FIX:
In your Next.js layout or page metadata generation (likely in
generateMetadata() or your layout.tsx), ensure each page passes
its own specific values for OG tags.

For the percentage calculator page, the correct tags should be:

  <meta property="og:url"
    content="https://calcfort.com/calculator/mathematics/percentage" />
  <meta property="og:title"
    content="Percentage Calculator — Percent Change & Difference" />
  <meta property="og:description"
    content="Calculate percentages, percent increase, decrease and
    change between numbers quickly and accurately." />

The og:url must match the canonical URL of each individual page.
The og:title should match the page <title> tag.
The og:description should match the page meta description.

In Next.js App Router using generateMetadata(), this looks like:

  export async function generateMetadata({ params }) {
    return {
      openGraph: {
        title: pageTitle,         // page-specific
        description: pageDesc,    // page-specific
        url: "https://calcfort.com/calculator/" + params.category
             + "/" + params.slug,
      },
    };
  }

Do this for ALL page types:
  - /calculator/[category]/[slug]
  - /learn/[category]/[slug]
  - /calculator/[category]  (category index pages)
  - /learn  (learn index page)
  - All other pages

EXPECTED OUTCOME:
Google can now distinguish each page as a unique URL. The
Google-selected canonical field will populate. Pages become
eligible for indexing. This single fix is the most impactful
change you can make.

ISSUE 3 — FAKE AGGREGATE RATINGS IN STRUCTURED DATA
Priority: HIGH — Fix Before Requesting Re-indexing
----------------------------------------------------------------

WHAT IS WRONG:
Every calculator page contains this JSON-LD schema:

  "aggregateRating": {
    "@type": "AggregateRating",
    "ratingValue": "4.8",
    "ratingCount": "1250",
    "bestRating": "5",
    "worstRating": "1"
  }

There is no visible rating mechanism on any page. A site founded
in 2024 cannot have 1,250 genuine user ratings per calculator.
Google's quality systems and the March 2024 spam update
specifically target fake structured data as a spam signal. If a
manual reviewer or automated system flags this, it can trigger a
site-wide quality demotion that is very difficult to recover from.

HOW TO FIX:
Remove the aggregateRating block entirely from all pages.

Do not replace it with lower numbers — any hardcoded rating
without a real review system is still fake and still a risk.

If you want to add real ratings in future, implement an actual
user rating widget that stores and displays genuine submissions,
then add the schema only once you have real data.

EXPECTED OUTCOME:
Removal of a significant spam signal from all 222 pages.
Reduces risk of manual penalty. Improves structured data
validity scores.

ISSUE 4 — AUTHOR / E-E-A-T SIGNALS MISSING
Priority: MEDIUM — Implement After Fixing Issues 1-3
----------------------------------------------------------------

WHAT IS WRONG:
No author attribution exists on any page. Google's quality
systems (E-E-A-T: Experience, Expertise, Authoritativeness,
Trustworthiness) require a credible human identity behind content.
A site with 150+ pages covering mathematics, finance, engineering,
space, chemistry, and energy — all with no named author — matches
the profile of automated content farms Google actively suppresses.

YOUR SITUATION:
You have a PhD in Electrical and Electronic Engineering and
professional experience at a major semiconductor company. This
makes you genuinely credible across mathematics, engineering
tools, and technical content. You do not need to use your real
name — a consistent pen name with a truthful bio is sufficient.

HOW TO FIX (OPTION A — Pen Name, Recommended):

1. Choose a pen name. Suggestion: "Ben Konna" or similar.

2. Create an author page at: calcfort.com/about/ben-konna
   Content should include (all truthful, nothing identifying):

   "Ben holds a PhD in Electrical and Electronic Engineering and
   has spent over a decade working in technical IC design and
   implementation. He writes about mathematics, engineering
   tools, and practical calculation methods, drawing on deep
   experience with complex technical systems and real-world
   engineering problems."

3. Add author attribution to every article and calculator page:
   - Visible byline: "By Ben Konna"
   - Link the byline to the author page
   - Add Person schema to the author page:

   {
     "@context": "https://schema.org",
     "@type": "Person",
     "name": "Ben Konna",
     "url": "https://calcfort.com/about/ben-konna",
     "description": "PhD engineer specialising in mathematics
       and technical implementation"
   }

HOW TO FIX (OPTION B — Brand Author, Simpler):

1. Use "The CalcFort Team" as the author on all pages.

2. Create a proper /about page that describes:
   - The team's background in engineering and mathematics
   - The purpose and approach of the site
   - Contact information

3. This is weaker than a named person but significantly better
   than no author at all.

EXPECTED OUTCOME:
Establishes E-E-A-T signals. Gives Google a credible identity
to attribute content to. Reduces the "automated content farm"
profile of the site.

ISSUE 5 — AD SCRIPTS LOADING BEFORE CONTENT
Priority: LOW — Address After Core Issues Fixed
----------------------------------------------------------------

WHAT IS WRONG:
Multiple heavy third-party scripts load in the <head> before
page content:
  - Ezoic (sa.min.js)
  - Gatekeeper Consent (min.js and cmp.min.js)
  - id5-sync API
  - Google Tag Manager (loaded via requestIdleCallback, which
    is better, but other scripts still block)

For a new site with no established authority, heavy ad
infrastructure loading on every page before the site has proven
its value to Google sends a poor Page Experience signal. Ezoic
in particular is known to interfere with Googlebot rendering
in some configurations.

HOW TO FIX:
1. Ensure ALL third-party scripts use defer or async attributes
   and load after the main content is parsed.
2. Move non-critical scripts to load on user interaction or
   after a delay (requestIdleCallback pattern — you already
   do this for GTM, apply it to others).
3. Consider whether Ezoic monetisation is worth keeping while
   the site is still trying to establish indexing. Ezoic
   requires a minimum traffic threshold anyway — it may be
   adding render complexity with no revenue benefit at this
   stage.
4. Test Page Experience scores at:
   https://pagespeed.web.dev/

EXPECTED OUTCOME:
Improved Core Web Vitals. Cleaner rendering for Googlebot.
Reduced risk of render-blocking issues affecting content
visibility.

ISSUE 6 — SITE-WIDE CONTENT QUALITY SIGNALS
Priority: MEDIUM — Ongoing, Run in Parallel
----------------------------------------------------------------

WHAT IS WRONG:
Google's Helpful Content System (integrated into core ranking
since March 2024) applies site-wide quality signals. Your site
currently matches several patterns it flags:

  - Large volume of pages published rapidly across many
    unrelated topic domains
  - Identical content structure across all pages
  - No unique data, original research, or first-hand experience
  - Content that exists primarily to funnel users to a tool
    rather than to inform independently

HOW TO FIX (Long-term content strategy):

1. Pick 2-3 topic areas where your background gives you
   genuine authority. Given your engineering PhD and
   experience, the strongest candidates are:
     - Mathematics & calculation tools
     - Engineering & electronics calculators
   These align with verifiable expertise. Energy/finance/space
   are weaker without domain-specific credentials.

2. For your highest-priority calculator pages, add something
   no other site has — a real example from engineering practice,
   a note about how this calculation is used in IC design, or
   a specific insight from professional experience. Even one
   paragraph of genuine expert perspective per page changes
   the quality signal significantly.

3. Consider removing or noindexing the weakest pages — "Space
   Discoveries 2026", "A Day on Mars", and similar content
   that has no connection to your site's core purpose and no
   credible expertise behind it. These pages actively drag
   down your domain's quality score.

4. The /learn/ articles that exist purely to explain your own
   calculators are low-value in Google's eyes. Either make
   them genuinely useful independent resources (not just
   "here's how to use our calculator") or noindex them and
   focus Google's attention on the calculator pages themselves.

EXPECTED OUTCOME:
Gradual improvement in site-wide quality signals. Better
alignment between content and demonstrable expertise. Reduced
risk of Helpful Content System suppression.
