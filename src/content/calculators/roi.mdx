## How to Use the ROI Calculator
The **ROI calculator** helps you evaluate the profitability of any investment by computing **simple ROI, annualized ROI, and payback period** in seconds. Simply enter your total investment cost and expected gains (or annual cash flows), and the tool instantly quantifies your return. It also supports **NPV and IRR calculations** for complex, multi-year projects with uneven cash flows. This empowers you to compare marketing campaigns, equipment purchases, or business initiatives on a level playing field—ensuring every pound you spend delivers maximum value.

### Key Formulas Used
- **Simple ROI**: `[(Gain – Cost) / Cost] × 100`
- **Annualized ROI**: `[(1 + Total ROI)^(1/n) – 1] × 100`
- **Payback Period**: `Initial Investment / Annual Cash Inflow`
- **NPV**: `Σ [Cash Flow_t / (1 + r)^t] – Initial Investment`

### Best Practices for Accurate Results
1.  **Include all costs**: Factor in hidden expenses like training, maintenance, and staff time.
2.  **Use conservative estimates**: Overly optimistic gains inflate ROI—use realistic projections.
3.  **Set a hurdle rate**: Compare your ROI to your minimum acceptable return (e.g., 10–15%).
4.  **For long-term projects**, use annualized ROI or NPV—simple ROI ignores time value.
5.  **Run multiple scenarios**: Test best/worst-case assumptions to gauge risk.

## Step-by-Step Example
You’re considering a £18,000 software subscription that will save 10 hours/week of staff time (£50/hour).

**Step 1**: Calculate annual benefit → 10 × 50 × 52 = **£26,000**  
**Step 2**: Enter into calculator:  
- Investment Cost: £18,000  
- Annual Gain: £26,000  
- Project Life: 3 years  

**Results**:  
- **Simple ROI**: (78000 – 18000) / 18000 = **333%**  
- **Annualized ROI**: (4.33)^(1/3) – 1 ≈ **62.5%**  
- **Payback Period**: 18000 / 26000 ≈ **0.69 years (8.3 months)**  

With a hurdle rate of 20%, this is a **highly attractive investment**.

### How do I calculate ROI for a project with multiple years of cash flow?
Use the **NPV/IRR mode**. Enter your initial investment as a negative cash flow (Year 0), then input each year’s net cash inflow. The calculator will compute NPV (using your hurdle rate) and IRR for a rigorous analysis.

### What’s the difference between simple and annualized ROI?
- **Simple ROI** shows total return over the project’s life (e.g., 50% over 5 years).  
- **Annualized ROI** shows the equivalent yearly return (e.g., 8.4% per year).  
Always use **annualized ROI** to compare projects of different durations.

### Can I use this for personal investments?
Yes—for home renovations, education costs, or even stock purchases. Just input your personal cost of capital as the hurdle rate (e.g., 7% for a balanced portfolio).

### How accurate is the payback period for uneven cash flows?
The calculator uses **cumulative cash flow interpolation** for precision. For example, if you recover 90% of your investment in Year 2 and 110% in Year 3, it calculates the exact month of payback.

### Why is my NPV negative but ROI positive?
This happens when returns are **delayed**. ROI ignores timing, but NPV discounts future cash flows. A project with late payoffs may have positive ROI but negative NPV if the hurdle rate is high.

### What is a hurdle rate, and how do I set it?
Your **hurdle rate** is the minimum return you require to justify an investment. Set it based on:
- Your cost of capital (e.g., loan interest rate)
- Risk premium (add 5–10% for high-risk projects)
- Opportunity cost (return from alternative investments)

### Can I compare marketing channels using this?
Absolutely. Calculate ROI for each channel:
- **Cost**: Ad spend + creative + labour  
- **Gain**: Attributable profit (revenue × margin)  
The channel with the highest ROI deserves more budget.

### Does this account for taxes?
Not automatically. For after-tax ROI, input **net cash flows** (after tax). For corporate projects, use pre-tax cash flows and a pre-tax hurdle rate for consistency.
