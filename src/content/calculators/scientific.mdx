## Scientific Calculator — Trig, Logs, Exponents & More
Our online scientific calculator replicates the functionality of physical models like the Casio fx-83 or TI-30X, with a clean, intuitive interface. It supports **trigonometric functions**, **logarithms**, **exponents**, **combinatorics**, and **scientific notation**, all while enforcing correct **order of operations**. Perfect for students, engineers, and anyone tackling advanced maths, physics, or chemistry problems.

### Key Features
- **Angle Modes**: Switch between **DEG** (degrees) and **RAD** (radians)  
- **Scientific Notation**: Use the **EE** key for efficient entry (e.g., `6.02 EE 23`)  
- **Memory Functions**: Store, recall, and clear values with **M+**, **MR**, **MC**  
- **History**: Review and reuse past calculations  
- **Error Prevention**: Parentheses auto-balance and syntax highlighting

### Common Uses
- Solving equations with **sin**, **cos**, **tan**  
- Calculating **log**, **ln**, **10ˣ**, **eˣ**  
- Computing **factorials**, **nPr**, **nCr**

- Verifying homework or exam prep

This tool is ideal for GCSE, A-level, IB, AP, and university-level coursework.

## Step-by-Step: Calculating pH from Hydrogen Ion Concentration
**Scenario**: Find the pH of a solution with `[H⁺] = 2.5 × 10^-⁴ M`.  
*(Formula: `pH = −log[H⁺]`)*

1. **Enter the concentration**: `2.5 EE (−)4`  
2. **Press `log`**: Result ≈ `−3.602`  
3. **Press `(−)` or `+/−`**: Final result = **3.602**

**Result**: pH ≈ **3.60** (acidic)

Try other chemistry calculations:  
- **pOH**: `pOH = −log[OH⁻]`  
- **Antilog**: `10^(−pH)` to find `[H⁺]`

### How do I switch between degrees and radians?
Click the **DEG/RAD** toggle above the display. Always check this before trig calculations—using the wrong mode is the most common error.

### How do I enter scientific notation?
Use the **EE** key (not `×10^`). For `1.6 × 10^-¹⁹`, type: `1.6 EE (−)19`. The `(−)` key is for negative exponents.

### Why is `−3²` giving −9 instead of 9?
Because the calculator interprets it as `−(3²)`. For `(−3)²`, type: `( (−)3 ) x²`.

### Can I use parentheses?
Yes! The calculator supports nested parentheses for complex expressions like `(2 + 3) × (4 − 1)`.

### What does the `ANS` key do?
It recalls the **last result**. After calculating `5! = 120`, press `ANS ÷ 10` to get `12`.

### How do I calculate a square root?
Press the **√** key, then enter the number. For cube roots, use `x^(1/3)`.

### Is this calculator exam-compliant?
It mirrors approved physical models (e.g., Casio fx-83), but **online tools aren’t allowed in exams**. Use this for homework and practice only.

### How do I clear everything?
Press **AC** (All Clear) to reset the current calculation, or **C** to clear the last entry.

### Can I calculate combinations?
Yes! Use **nCr**: For "8 choose 3", type `8 nCr 3` → **56**.

### Why does `sin(30)` give −0.988?
You’re in **RAD** mode. Switch to **DEG** for `sin(30°) = 0.5`.
