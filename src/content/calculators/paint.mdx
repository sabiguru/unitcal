Our **paint calculator** helps you estimate exactly how much paint and primer you need for any room or project. By inputting your room dimensions, number of doors/windows, and paint specifications, you’ll get a precise shopping list that accounts for walls, ceilings, trim, multiple coats, and a waste buffer. Whether you’re refreshing a bedroom or tackling your entire home, this tool prevents costly overbuying and frustrating mid-project shortages.

### How It Works
The calculator uses standard painting formulas:
- **Wall area**: `2 × (Length + Width) × Height`
- **Net area**: Subtract doors/windows (21 sq ft per door, 16 sq ft per window)
- **Paint needed**: `(Net Area ÷ Coverage per Gallon) × Coats × 1.10` (10% waste buffer)

It handles:
- **Multiple surfaces**: Walls, ceilings, and trim
- **Custom openings**: Adjust for non-standard doors/windows
- **Coverage rates**: 250–400 sq ft/gallon based on surface texture
- **Primer estimates**: Separate calculation for sealing and base coats

### Key Inputs
- **Room dimensions**: Length, width, height
- **Openings**: Number and size of doors/windows
- **Paint coverage**: From your paint can label (default: 375 sq ft/gallon)
- **Coats**: Typically 2 for walls/ceiling, 1 for trim
- **Surface type**: Smooth, textured, or exterior

The tool instantly shows:
- Gallons of paint needed
- Gallons of primer (if selected)
- Total cost estimate (if paint price is entered)
- Shopping list with recommended quantities

### How to Use
1. Enter your **room dimensions** (length, width, height)
2. Add **doors and windows** (standard sizes or custom)
3. Select **surface type** (smooth, textured, etc.)
4. Input your **paint’s coverage rate** (from the can)
5. Choose **number of coats** and whether to include **primer**
6. Click **Calculate** to get your precise paint estimate

Always round up to the nearest gallon—paint batches vary, and you’ll want extra for touch-ups.

### Step-by-Step Example
**Scenario**: Painting a 12×10 ft bedroom with 8 ft ceilings.

**Inputs**:
- Length: 12 ft
- Width: 10 ft
- Height: 8 ft
- Doors: 1 (standard)
- Windows: 1 (standard)
- Coverage: 375 sq ft/gallon
- Coats: 2 (walls/ceiling), 1 (trim)
- Surface: Smooth

**Calculation**:
1. **Wall area**: `2 × (12 + 10) × 8 = 352 sq ft`
2. **Openings**: 21 + 16 = 37 sq ft
3. **Net wall area**: 352 – 37 = 315 sq ft
4. **Ceiling**: 12 × 10 = 120 sq ft
5. **Total area**: 315 + 120 = 435 sq ft
6. **Paint needed**: `(435 ÷ 375) × 2 = 2.32 gallons`
7. **With 10% buffer**: 2.32 × 1.1 = **2.55 gallons**

**Result**: **Buy 3 gallons** of paint. If adding trim (25 sq ft), total becomes **2.63 gallons**—still **3 gallons**.

This ensures you have enough for two flawless coats and future touch-ups.

### How accurate is this calculator?
It’s highly accurate if you input correct dimensions and coverage rates. The 10% waste buffer accounts for real-world variables like spillage and surface texture.

### What if my room isn’t rectangular?
Calculate each wall separately: measure width and height of each wall, multiply to get area, then sum all walls. The calculator allows manual entry of total wall area if needed.

### Do I need to include the ceiling?
Only if you’re painting it. Ceilings typically require 1–2 coats and add significantly to the total area (Length × Width).

### Should I buy primer?
Yes—if you’re painting new drywall, covering stains, or making a drastic color change. The calculator provides a separate primer estimate based on net wall area.

### What’s the standard coverage rate?
Most quality interior paints cover **350–400 sq ft/gallon** on smooth, primed surfaces. For textured walls, use **250–300 sq ft/gallon**.

### Why do I need two coats?
Two coats ensure uniform color, complete coverage (especially over previous colors), and a consistent sheen. One coat often looks patchy and translucent.

### Can I return unused paint?
Most stores accept returns of **unopened** paint. **Opened** paint is usually non-returnable—so the 10% buffer is for touch-ups, not overbuying.

### How do I measure high ceilings?
For vaulted or cathedral ceilings, break walls into rectangular and triangular sections. Calculate each separately, then sum the areas.

### Does this work for exterior painting?
Yes—just adjust the coverage rate to **250–350 sq ft/gallon** for exterior surfaces, which are typically more porous and textured.
