### Step-by-Step Example
**Scenario**: Multiply `3/5 × 5/12`.

**Inputs**:
- Fraction 1: 3/5
- Fraction 2: 5/12
- Operation: Multiply

**Calculation Process**:
1. **Cross-cancel**: The 5 in the numerator of the second fraction cancels with the 5 in the denominator of the first:  
   `3/5 × 5/12 → 3/1 × 1/12`
2. **Multiply**: `(3 × 1) / (1 × 12) = 3/12`
3. **Simplify**: GCD of 3 and 12 is 3 → `3÷3 / 12÷3 = 1/4`

**Result**: The product is **1/4**.

This shows how cross-canceling simplifies the process and prevents large numbers.

### How do I add fractions with different denominators?
The calculator finds the **Least Common Multiple (LCM)** of the denominators, converts both fractions to equivalent forms with that denominator, adds the numerators, and simplifies the result.

### What is cross-canceling?
**Cross-canceling** is simplifying before multiplying by dividing a numerator and a denominator (from different fractions) by their common factor. It makes multiplication easier and avoids large numbers.

### Can I use mixed numbers?
Yes. Enter mixed numbers like “2 3/4”. The calculator converts them to improper fractions for the operation, then displays the result as a mixed number if applicable.

### Why does the calculator simplify fractions?
Simplified fractions are in **lowest terms**, which is the standard mathematical form. It makes results easier to interpret and compare (e.g., 1/2 is clearer than 4/8).

### Does this work for negative fractions?
Yes. Enter negative signs with the numerator (e.g., “-3/4”). The calculator handles signs correctly in all operations.

### How is this different from a regular calculator?
A regular calculator gives **decimal approximations** (e.g., 0.333 for 1/3), which are rounded and imprecise. This calculator gives **exact fractional answers** (1/3), which are mathematically accurate.

### Can I divide by a whole number?
Yes. Enter the whole number as a fraction (e.g., 4 = 4/1). The calculator will handle it correctly.

### Is there a limit to the size of fractions?
No. The calculator handles large numerators and denominators, though extremely large values may slow down simplification.
