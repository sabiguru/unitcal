## What is an Aquarium Volume Calculator?

An **aquarium volume calculator** is an essential tool that determines the exact amount of water your fish tank holds based on its shape and internal dimensions. Knowing this volume is critical for the health of your aquarium. It ensures you dose water conditioners, fertilizers, and medications correctly, choose the right size filter and heater, and confirm that your stand and floor can safely support the tank's total weight. This calculator provides instant, accurate results for a wide range of tank shapes.

## The Science Behind the Calculation

The calculator uses precise geometric formulas tailored to each tank shape. The core principle is to calculate the internal space's capacity in cubic units and then convert that value into practical units like gallons or litres. For complex shapes like bow fronts, it uses advanced approximations to ensure a reliable result. It also estimates the water weight, a crucial factor for safety.

## How to Use This Calculator

Getting your tank's volume and weight is a simple, three-step process.

1.  **Select Your Tank Shape:** Choose your aquarium's shape from the options (e.g., **Rectangle, Cylinder, Bow Front, Hexagon**).
2.  **Enter Your Dimensions:** Carefully measure and input the **internal** dimensions of your tank. Use a tape measure for accuracy. You can usually specify units (inches or centimetres).
    *   **For Rectangle:** Length, Width, Height.
    *   **For Cylinder:** Diameter, Height.
    *   **For Bow Front/Hexagon:** The required measurements will be shown.
3.  **Click "Calculate":** The tool will process the inputs and display the results instantly.
4.  **Review Your Results:** You will see the water volume in multiple units (US Gallons, Imperial Gallons, Litres) and an estimate of the water weight.

### Pro Tip
For the most accurate volume for *dosing*, measure the actual water height from the top of the substrate to the waterline, not the full tank height. This accounts for the space taken up by your substrate.

## A Step-by-Step Example

**Scenario:** You have a standard rectangular tank and need to know its volume to dose a medication.

1.  **Select Shape:** Choose **"Rectangle"**.
2.  **Input Dimensions:** You measure the inside of the tank:
    *   Length: `24 inches`
    *   Width: `12 inches`
    *   Height (water depth): `15 inches`
3.  **Calculation:** The calculator performs the math:
    *   Cubic Inches: `24 x 12 x 15 = 4,320 in³`
    *   US Gallons: `4,320 / 231 ≈ 18.7 gallons`
    *   Water Weight: `18.7 x 8.34 ≈ 156 lbs`
4.  **Result:** The tool tells you your tank holds **18.7 US gallons** of water, weighing approximately **156 pounds**. You would use the 18.7-gallon figure to calculate the correct medication dose.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Q: Why is the actual water volume in my tank less than the calculated volume?**
A: The calculated volume is the maximum capacity. The actual water volume is reduced by the displacement caused by substrate (gravel, sand), decorations, rocks, and internal equipment. It's common for the actual water volume to be 10-15% less in a decorated tank.

**Q: How important is it to calculate the total weight?**
A: Extremely important for safety. Water is very heavy (8.34 lbs per US gallon). A full aquarium's weight includes the water, tank, substrate, and decor. This total weight must be within the safe load capacity of your furniture and floor to prevent accidents.

**Q: Should I use US gallons or Imperial gallons?**
A: This depends on your location and the products you use. Most aquarium products in the US are dosed per **US gallon**. The UK and Canada often use **Imperial gallons**. Our calculator provides both, but you should use the unit specified on your water treatment products.

**Q: How do I measure a bow front aquarium?**
A: For a bow front, you typically need to measure the full length, the full height, and the width at the widest point (the front glass's chord) and sometimes the depth of the bow. Our calculator will prompt you for the specific measurements needed for an accurate calculation.

**Q: Can I use this calculator for a pond?**
A: The principles are the same for rectangular or circular ponds. For irregular pond shapes, you may need to break the shape down into multiple regular sections (rectangles, circles) and calculate each volume separately before adding them together.

**Q: What's the difference between 'height' and 'filled height'?**
A: 'Height' is the internal dimension of the empty tank. 'Filled height' is the actual depth of the water, which is less if you have substrate or don't fill the tank to the brim. Using the 'filled height' gives a more accurate water volume for dosing.
