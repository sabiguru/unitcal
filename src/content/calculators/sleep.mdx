## Introduction
Our **sleep calculator** helps you plan bedtimes and wake times based on your body’s natural **90-minute sleep cycles**. By waking at the end of a light sleep phase—not during deep sleep—you’ll reduce grogginess and start your day feeling refreshed.

### How It Works
The calculator uses two key inputs:
- Your **desired wake time** (or bedtime)
- Your estimated **time to fall asleep** (typically 10–20 minutes)

It then calculates optimal bedtimes (or wake times) for **4, 5, or 6 sleep cycles** (6, 7.5, or 9 hours of sleep), ensuring you rise at the end of a cycle.

### How to Use
1. Choose whether you’re setting a **wake time** or **bedtime**
2. Enter your time (e.g., `7:00 AM`)
3. Enter your **sleep onset** (time to fall asleep)
4. The tool shows recommended times for 4–6 cycles
5. Pick the option that fits your schedule and lifestyle

This tool is ideal for students, shift workers, parents, and anyone seeking better morning alertness.

## Step-by-Step Example
**Scenario**: You need to wake at **6:30 AM** and usually take **15 minutes** to fall asleep. You’d like to get a full 7.5 hours (5 cycles).

**Steps**:
1. Select **“Wake Time”** as your input type
2. Enter `6:30 AM` as your wake time
3. Enter `15` minutes for sleep onset

   - **4 cycles (6 hrs)**: Bedtime = **12:15 AM**

✅ **Result**: Aim to be in bed with lights out by **10:45 PM** to wake refreshed at **6:30 AM**.

<TabPanel title="FAQ">
### Why 90 minutes?
Sleep occurs in ~90-minute cycles of light, deep, and REM sleep. Waking at the end of a cycle (during light sleep) minimises grogginess.

### How many cycles do I need?
Most adults need **5–6 cycles (7.5–9 hours)**. Start with 5 and adjust based on how you feel during the day.

### What if I don’t fall asleep in the time I entered?
The calculator assumes your estimate is accurate. If you’re often wrong, adjust your sleep onset input (e.g., from 15 to 25 minutes).

### Can I use this for naps?
Yes! For naps, choose either:
- **20–30 minutes** (power nap, avoids deep sleep)
- **90 minutes** (one full cycle)

Avoid 45–60 minute naps—they often end in deep sleep, causing inertia.

### Does this work for shift workers?
Absolutely. Enter your intended **daytime sleep window** and the calculator will suggest optimal wake times based on the same 90-minute principle.

### What if I wake up before my alarm?
That’s a good sign! It likely means you completed your cycles and are in a light sleep phase. Note that time—it may reflect your natural rhythm.

### Is this a medical tool?
No. This is for general wellness planning only. If you have chronic insomnia, sleep apnea, or excessive daytime fatigue, consult a healthcare professional.
