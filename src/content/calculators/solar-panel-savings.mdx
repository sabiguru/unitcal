## How to Use This Calculator - Solar Panel Savings
Enter your System Size in kilowatts peak (kWp)—typical UK residential systems are 3-4 kWp.

Select your Roof Orientation: South (best), East, West, or North.

Enter your Electricity Rate in pence per kWh (check your bill; typical is £0.28/kWh).

Enter your SEG (Smart Export Guarantee) Rate—what you're paid for exported electricity (typical £0.05-0.15/kWh).

Enter your Self-Consumption Percentage—how much solar energy you use directly (typical 30-40% without battery).

## The Core Principle: Energy Production and Savings

Solar savings come from two sources: using solar energy yourself (avoiding grid purchases) and selling excess to the grid.

**Annual production formula:**
Production (kWh) = System Size (kWp) × Location Factor × Orientation Factor × 1,000

In the UK, a south-facing system produces approximately 850-950 kWh per kWp annually.

**Self-consumption savings:**
Self-Use Savings = Production × Self-Consumption % × Electricity Rate

**Export income:**
Export Income = Production × (1 - Self-Consumption %) × SEG Rate

**Total savings:**
Total = Self-Use Savings + Export Income

Self-consumption is more valuable than export because electricity rates far exceed SEG rates. A battery increases self-consumption significantly.

## How to Calculate Solar Savings Manually

**Example: 4 kWp south-facing system in Midlands**

**Step 1: Annual production**
4 kWp × 876 kWh/kWp (Midlands average) = 3,504 kWh/year

**Step 2: Self-consumption savings (35%)**
3,504 × 0.35 × £0.28 = £343/year

**Step 3: Export income (65%)**
3,504 × 0.65 × £0.07 = £159/year

**Step 4: Total annual savings**
£343 + £159 = £502/year

**Step 5: Payback period**
System cost £8,000 ÷ £502/year = 15.9 years

**Step 6: 25-year return**
£502 × 25 = £12,550 savings over system lifetime
Net profit: £12,550 - £8,000 = £4,550

This ignores electricity price inflation, which typically makes actual returns higher.

## Scenarios People Actually Run Into

**The orientation reality check.** East or west facing roofs produce about 85% of south-facing output. North-facing produces about 65%—still viable but with longer payback.

**The self-consumption challenge.** Most households use 30-40% of solar production without a battery because peak production (midday) doesn't align with peak usage (morning/evening).

**The export rate disappointment.** SEG rates (£0.05-0.15/kWh) are much lower than electricity purchase rates (£0.25-0.35/kWh). Maximizing self-consumption is crucial.

**The shading surprise.** Even partial shading significantly reduces output. Trees, chimneys, and neighboring buildings can impact production substantially.

**The winter reality.** UK solar produces 70-80% of annual output between April and September. Winter months produce much less.

---

## Trade-Offs and Decisions People Underestimate

**Self-consumption versus system size.** A larger system produces more but has lower percentage self-consumption. Balance system size with realistic usage patterns.

**Battery economics.** Batteries increase self-consumption dramatically but add significant cost. Payback for batteries alone is often longer than for panels alone.

**Panel quality versus cost.** Premium panels cost more but may have better warranties, longer life, and higher efficiency. Consider total lifetime value.

**Installation quality.** Cheap installations may have problems. Quality installers ensure proper mounting, wiring, and grid connection.

**Future electricity prices.** Rising electricity prices improve solar economics. Falling prices (unlikely but possible) reduce returns.

## Common Mistakes and How to Recover

**Overestimating self-consumption.** Without a battery, 30-40% is realistic. Using 70% typically requires a battery or significant daytime usage.

**Ignoring orientation impact.** South-facing is optimal. East/west reduces output to ~85%. North-facing to ~65%. Factor this into calculations.

**Forgetting maintenance costs.** Inverters typically need replacement at 10-15 years (£500-1,000). Budget for this.

**Assuming constant export rates.** SEG rates vary by supplier and may change over time. Use conservative estimates.

**Ignoring shading.** Even partial shading has disproportionate impact. Get a professional shading analysis.

## Related Topics

**Smart Export Guarantee (SEG).** UK scheme requiring suppliers to pay for exported solar electricity.

**Battery storage.** Stores excess solar for evening use, dramatically increasing self-consumption.

**Feed-in Tariff (FIT).** Previous UK scheme (closed 2019) that paid more for solar generation. Existing FIT customers still receive payments.

**Grid parity.** When solar electricity costs equal or less than grid electricity, making solar economically compelling.

**Net metering.** System where exported electricity offsets imported electricity (not available in UK; SEG is different).

## How This Calculator Works

**Annual production:**
```
baseProduction = systemSize × regionFactor × 1000

orientationMultipliers = {
  south: 1.00,
  eastWest: 0.85,
  north: 0.65
}

annualProduction = baseProduction × orientationMultiplier
```

**Self-consumption savings:**
```
selfUsed = annualProduction × selfConsumptionPercent
selfSavings = selfUsed × electricityRate
```

**Export income:**
```
exported = annualProduction × (1 - selfConsumptionPercent)
exportIncome = exported × segRate
```

**Financial metrics:**
```
totalAnnualSavings = selfSavings + exportIncome
paybackYears = systemCost / totalAnnualSavings
lifetimeReturn = totalAnnualSavings × 25
roi = (lifetimeReturn - systemCost) / systemCost × 100
```

All calculations happen locally in your browser.

## FAQs

**How accurate is this calculator?**

It uses PVWatts methodology adapted for UK conditions. Results are good estimates; professional site surveys provide more precision.

**What is self-consumption?**

The percentage of solar energy you use directly rather than exporting. Higher self-consumption means greater savings.

**Do I need a smart meter for SEG?**

Yes. A smart meter is required to measure exported electricity and receive SEG payments.

**How does roof orientation affect output?**

South is optimal (100%). East/West produces ~85%. North produces ~65%. Even north-facing can be viable.

**Can I include battery storage in calculations?**

Not directly, but you can increase self-consumption percentage (to 60-70%) to model battery effects.

**Are there hidden costs?**

Standard installation costs include most work. Additional costs may include scaffolding (£300-600) and electrical upgrades if needed.

**How long do solar panels last?**

Panels typically last 25-30 years with gradual output decline (~0.5% per year). Inverters last 10-15 years.

**Does solar work in the UK?**

Yes. The UK receives sufficient sunlight for viable solar. A 4kWp system produces approximately 3,400-3,800 kWh annually.
