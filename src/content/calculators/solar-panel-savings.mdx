## Introduction
Our **solar panel savings calculator** helps UK homeowners estimate the financial benefits of installing solar PV. By entering your system size, location, electricity tariff, and export rate, it calculates your **annual savings**, **payback period**, and **25-year return**—so you can make an informed investment decision.

### How It Works
The calculator uses UK-specific solar irradiance data and standard assumptions to model:
- **Annual energy production** (kWh) based on system size and roof orientation
- **Self-consumption savings** (using your electricity rate)
- **Export income** (via Smart Export Guarantee rates)
- **Total annual savings** and **payback period**

### How to Use
1. Enter your **system size** (e.g., 4 kWp)
2. Select your **roof orientation** (South, East, West, etc.)
3. Input your **electricity rate** (e.g., £0.28/kWh)
4. Enter your **SEG export rate** (e.g., £0.08/kWh)
5. Adjust **self-consumption %** (default: 40%)
6. The tool shows **annual savings**, **payback years**, and **lifetime ROI**

This calculator is ideal for comparing quotes, planning battery additions, or understanding how energy price changes affect your savings.

<TabPanel title="FAQ">
### How accurate is this calculator?
It uses **PVWatts methodology** adapted for UK weather and MCS standards. It’s excellent for initial estimates but not a substitute for a professional site survey.

### What is self-consumption?
The percentage of solar energy you **use directly** in your home. Typical UK homes use 30–40% without a battery. Higher self-consumption = greater savings.

### Do I need a smart meter for SEG?
Yes. A **smart meter** is required to measure exported electricity and receive SEG payments from your supplier.

### How does roof orientation affect output?
- **South**: Maximum output (100%)
- **East/West**: ~85% of south-facing
- **North**: ~65% — still viable, but longer payback

### Can I include battery storage in this calculator?
Not directly. But you can **increase your self-consumption %** (e.g., to 60–70%) to model the effect of a battery on savings.

### Are there hidden costs?
The calculator assumes a standard installation. Additional costs may include:
- Scaffolding (£300–£600)
- Inverter replacement after 10–15 years (£800–£1,200)
- Battery (if added later)

### Is solar worth it in Scotland or the North?
Yes! While output is 15–20% lower than southern England, high electricity prices and 0% VAT still make solar financially viable, with payback typically 16–18 years.

### How do I find my SEG rate?
Check your energy supplier’s website or use Ofgem’s SEG register. Rates range from **£0.04 to £0.15/kWh**—shop around annually for the best deal.
