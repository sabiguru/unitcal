## How to Use the Protein Calculator
The **protein calculator** helps you determine your optimal daily protein intake based on your weight, goals, and activity level. Whether you’re focused on **muscle gain, fat loss, or general health**, simply enter your weight and select your objective, and the tool instantly provides a personalised protein target in grams per day. It also breaks down your intake into practical per-meal portions and compares your needs to the official RDA, ensuring you’re not just meeting minimums—but optimising for results.

### Key Formulas Used
- **Weight in kg** = Weight in lbs ÷ 2.2
- **Daily Protein (g)** = Weight (kg) × Protein Factor (g/kg)
  - *Factors*: 0.8 (RDA), 1.6–2.4 (fat loss), 1.6–2.2 (muscle gain)

### Best Practices
1.  **Use current body weight**—not goal weight—for accurate calculations.
2.  **Choose your primary goal**—the calculator adjusts protein ranges accordingly.
3.  **Distribute intake evenly** across 3–4 meals for best muscle synthesis.
4.  **Combine with calorie tracking**—protein alone won’t drive fat loss or muscle gain without proper energy balance.

## Step-by-Step Example
You weigh **165 lbs** and want to **build muscle**.

**Step 1**: Convert to kg → 165 ÷ 2.2 = **75 kg**  
**Step 2**: Select goal → **Muscle Gain** (uses 1.8 g/kg)  
**Step 3**: Calculate → 75 × 1.8 = **135 g protein/day**  
**Step 4**: Per-meal target → 135 ÷ 4 = **34 g per meal**

The calculator also shows:
- **RDA comparison**: 75 × 0.8 = 60 g (you need **2.25× more**)
- **Food examples**: 5 oz chicken breast = ~35g protein

This gives you a clear, actionable target for meal planning.

<TabPanel title="FAQ">
### What if I’m overweight—should I use total or lean body weight?
For simplicity, use **total body weight**. Research shows it’s effective and easier to track. If you’re very high body fat (>30% men, >40% women), you can use **target weight** instead.

### Can I eat all my protein in one meal?
Technically yes—but your body can only utilise ~20–40g per meal for muscle synthesis. Spreading intake maximises benefits.

### Is plant protein as effective as animal protein?
Plant proteins are slightly less bioavailable and may lack some essential amino acids. To compensate, increase intake by 10–20% and combine sources (e.g., rice + beans).

### How does age affect protein needs?
After 65, protein needs increase to **1.2–2.0 g/kg** to combat sarcopenia (age-related muscle loss). Don’t stick to the RDA if you’re older.

### Should I adjust protein on rest days?
No—keep intake consistent daily. Muscle repair continues 24–48 hours post-workout.

### Can high protein cause weight gain?
Only if it pushes you into a calorie surplus. Protein itself is not fattening—but excess calories are.

### What’s the best protein source?
The best source is the one you’ll eat consistently. Prioritise **whole foods** (eggs, fish, legumes), and use **protein powder** for convenience.
