## Master the Japanese Soroban Abacus Online
The soroban is a traditional Japanese 1:4 abacus that builds lightning-fast mental arithmetic skills through tactile, visual learning. Unlike digital calculators, it teaches the *logic* behind numbers using bead manipulation and complement rules. Our interactive soroban lets you drag beads or input numbers directly to practise addition, subtraction, and place value in real time.

### How It Works
- **Heaven bead** (top): worth **5** when moved down to the beam  
- **Earth beads** (bottom): each worth **1** when moved up to the beam  
- **Each rod** = a place value (units, tens, hundreds…)  
- **Complement rules** (to 5 and 10) enable efficient carries/borrows  

### How to Use This Calculator
1. **Clear** the abacus (all beads away from beam)  
2. **Set a number** by dragging beads or typing in the input box  
3. **Perform calculations** by manipulating beads using complement logic  
4. **Read the result** from the digital display above  

This tool is perfect for students, educators, and anyone wanting to improve number sense and mental maths.

## Step-by-Step: Adding 47 + 68 on the Soroban
1. **Set 47**:  
   - Tens rod: 4 earth beads up (4)  
   - Units rod: 1 heaven bead down + 2 earth beads up (5 + 2 = 7)  
2. **Add 60**:  
   - Tens = 4 + 6 = 10 → **carry to hundreds**  
   - Clear tens rod, add 1 to hundreds rod  
3. **Add 8 to units**:  
   - Units = 7 + 8 = 15 → **carry again**  
   - Use complement: +8 = +10 – 2  
   - Add 1 to tens rod (now 1), subtract 2 from units (7 – 2 = 5)  
4. **Final result**: Hundreds = 1, Tens = 1, Units = 5 → **115**

### What is a 1:4 abacus?
A 1:4 abacus (like the soroban) has **one upper bead** (value 5) and **four lower beads** (value 1 each) per rod. This streamlined design is optimised for base-10 calculations and forces efficient use of complement rules.

### How do I clear the soroban?
Move all **upper beads up** and all **lower beads down** so no beads touch the central beam. The digital readout should show **0**.

### Can I use this for decimals?
Yes. Simply decide which rod represents the decimal point (e.g., the middle rod). All rods to its right are fractional (tenths, hundredths, etc.). The calculation rules remain unchanged.

### Why use a soroban instead of a calculator?
The soroban builds **number intuition**, **concentration**, and **mental calculation skills**—benefits a standard calculator cannot provide. It’s a cognitive workout, not just a tool for answers.

### Is this suitable for children?
Absolutely. The visual, hands-on nature makes abstract maths concrete. It’s widely used in Japanese schools to develop foundational arithmetic fluency and focus.

### How do I learn the complement rules?
Start with small numbers:  
- To add 6 when you have 4: **+6 = +10 – 4**  
- To subtract 7 from 2: **–7 = –10 + 3**
