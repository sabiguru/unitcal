Our **electrical calculator** helps you solve for voltage, current, resistance, and power in DC circuits using **Ohm’s Law** and **power formulas**. Whether you’re designing a simple LED circuit, sizing a resistor, or troubleshooting a power supply, this tool provides instant, accurate results to ensure your projects are safe and efficient.

### How It Works
The calculator uses the core electrical formulas:
- **Ohm’s Law**: `V = I × R`
- **Power**: `P = V × I = I² × R = V² / R`

For **series circuits**, it sums resistances. For **parallel circuits**, it calculates the reciprocal sum. You can also combine both in mixed networks.

### Step-by-Step Example
**Scenario**: Powering an LED with a 9V battery.

**Inputs**:
- Supply Voltage: 9V
- LED Forward Voltage: 2.1V
- Desired Current: 20mA (0.02A)

**Calculation**:
1. Voltage across resistor: `9V – 2.1V = 6.9V`
2. Resistor value: `R = V / I = 6.9V / 0.02A = 345Ω`
3. Power dissipation: `P = I² × R = (0.02)² × 345 = 0.138W`

**Result**: Use a **345Ω resistor** (or nearest standard 330Ω or 360Ω) rated for **at least 0.25W**.

This ensures the LED operates safely without burning out.

### What is Ohm’s Law?
Ohm’s Law states that **voltage = current × resistance** (`V = I × R`). It defines the relationship between these three fundamental electrical quantities in resistive circuits.

### How do I calculate total resistance?
- **Series**: Add all resistances: `R_total = R₁ + R₂ + ...`  
- **Parallel**: Use `1/R_total = 1/R₁ + 1/R₂ + ...`  
For two resistors in parallel: `R_total = (R₁ × R₂) / (R₁ + R₂)`

### What is the difference between real and apparent power?
**Real power (W)** does useful work (e.g., heat, light). **Apparent power (VA)** is the product of voltage and current. In AC circuits with inductive/capacitive loads, they differ due to **power factor**.

### Can I use this for AC circuits?
Yes—for **resistive AC loads** (heaters, incandescent bulbs), DC formulas apply using RMS values. For **reactive loads** (motors, transformers), enable AC mode and input power factor.

### How do I choose a resistor wattage?
Calculate power dissipation (`P = I² × R`) and select a resistor rated for **at least double** that value (e.g., 0.25W resistor for 0.1W load) for safety and longevity.

### Why is my calculation not matching my multimeter?
Possible reasons:  
- Component tolerances (±5% resistors)  
- Battery internal resistance  
- Temperature effects  
- Measurement error  
Always measure under actual operating conditions.

### Is this calculator safe for mains voltage?
No. This tool is for **low-voltage DC circuits** (e.g., batteries, USB power). **Never use it to design or troubleshoot mains AC circuits** without professional training and certification.

### What are common beginner mistakes?
- Confusing mA with A (e.g., 20mA = 0.02A)  
- Forgetting LED forward voltage  
- Using undersized resistors  
- Ignoring power dissipation  
- Reversing polarity on polarised components
