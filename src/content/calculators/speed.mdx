## Speed, Distance & Time Calculator
Our speed and velocity calculator solves for any one of the three variables—**speed**, **distance**, or **time**—as long as you know the other two. It automatically handles unit conversions between **mph, km/h, and m/s**, and correctly computes **true average speed** for multi-leg journeys, avoiding the common pitfall of simply averaging speeds.

### How It Works
- **Core Formula**: `Speed = Distance / Time`  
- **Rearrangements**:  
  - `Distance = Speed × Time`  
  - `Time = Distance / Speed`  
- **Average Speed**: Always calculated as **Total Distance ÷ Total Time**, never as an arithmetic mean.

### How to Use
1. Select the variable you want to calculate (speed, distance, or time).  
2. Enter the known values with their units.  
3. The calculator instantly computes the result with correct unit handling.  
4. For trips with multiple segments, use the “Add Leg” feature to get accurate average speed.

Perfect for **road trip planning**, **fitness tracking**, **physics homework**, and everyday curiosity.

## Step-by-Step: Calculating Trip Time
**Scenario**: You’re driving 180 miles at an average speed of 60 mph. How long will it take?

1. **Select “Time”** as the unknown.  
2. **Enter Distance**: 180 miles  
3. **Enter Speed**: 60 mph  
4. **Calculate**:  
   - Time = 180 ÷ 60 = **3 hours**

**Now add a stop**: If you take a 30-minute break, total trip time = **3 hours 30 minutes**.

Try it yourself—enter your own distance and speed to plan your next journey!

### How do I calculate average speed for a trip with stops?
Include **all time**—driving and stops—in the total time.  
Example: Drive 100 miles in 2 hours, plus 30-minute stop → Total Time = 2.5 hrs → Avg Speed = 100 ÷ 2.5 = **40 mph**.

### Can I mix units (e.g., km and mph)?
No—always use consistent units. The calculator will auto-convert if you select matching unit pairs (e.g., km with km/h), but don’t mix km with mph manually.

### Why is my average speed lower than expected?
Because average speed is **total distance ÷ total time**, including slow segments. A 10-minute traffic jam at 5 mph drastically reduces average speed, even after 50 minutes at 70 mph.

### Does this work for running or cycling?
Yes! Enter distance in km or miles and time in hours/minutes to get speed (km/h or mph) or pace (min/km or min/mile).

### How do I convert speed units?
Use the unit dropdowns. For example, input speed as **10 m/s**, and the calculator can display it as **36 km/h** or **22.4 mph**.

### What if I have multiple trip legs?
Use the “Add Leg” button to input each segment. The calculator sums total distance and total time, then computes the correct average speed.

### Is this suitable for physics problems?
Absolutely. It handles constant-speed kinematics problems with precision and proper unit management—ideal for GCSE, A-level, or introductory university physics.
