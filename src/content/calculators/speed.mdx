## The Core Principle: Speed Equals Distance Over Time

The fundamental relationship:

**Speed = Distance / Time**

Rearranged:
- **Distance = Speed × Time**
- **Time = Distance / Speed**

**Average speed for a journey:**
Average Speed = Total Distance / Total Time

Important: Average speed is NOT the arithmetic mean of speeds. If you drive 30 mph for 1 hour and 60 mph for 1 hour, your average speed is 45 mph. But if you drive 30 mph for a certain distance and 60 mph for the same distance, your average speed is 40 mph (harmonic mean).

**Unit conversions:**
- 1 mph = 1.609 km/h
- 1 m/s = 3.6 km/h = 2.237 mph
- 1 knot = 1.151 mph = 1.852 km/h

## How to Calculate Speed, Distance, and Time

**Calculate time:**
Distance: 180 miles
Speed: 60 mph

Time = 180 / 60 = 3 hours

**Calculate speed:**
Distance: 150 km
Time: 2.5 hours

Speed = 150 / 2.5 = 60 km/h

**Calculate distance:**
Speed: 50 mph
Time: 4.5 hours

Distance = 50 × 4.5 = 225 miles

**Average speed with stops:**
Drive 100 miles in 2 hours, then stop for 30 minutes.

Total distance: 100 miles
Total time: 2.5 hours
Average speed: 100 / 2.5 = 40 mph

Note: The stop counts toward total time, reducing average speed.

## Scenarios People Actually Run Into

**The traffic jam trap.** A 10-minute traffic jam at 5 mph dramatically reduces average speed, even after an hour at 70 mph.

**The arithmetic mean mistake.** Driving 30 mph to work and 60 mph home (same distance) doesn't average 45 mph—it averages 40 mph.

**The unit mismatch.** Mixing kilometers with miles or hours with minutes produces nonsense results. Always use consistent units.

**The "as the crow flies" error.** Road distance is longer than straight-line distance. GPS provides driving distance, not direct distance.

**The break calculation.** A 3-hour drive with a 30-minute break takes 3.5 hours total. Factor breaks into planning.

## Trade-Offs and Decisions People Underestimate

**Speed versus safety.** Higher speed reduces travel time but increases risk. The relationship is non-linear—small speed increases have outsized risk impacts.

**Speed versus fuel efficiency.** Most vehicles achieve optimal fuel efficiency at 45-65 mph. Higher speeds significantly reduce mpg.

**Average versus instantaneous speed.** Your speedometer shows instantaneous speed. Trip calculations need average speed, which is always lower due to stops and slowdowns.

**Time savings reality.** Driving 80 mph instead of 70 mph over 100 miles saves only about 11 minutes, while significantly increasing risk.

**Multi-modal comparisons.** Door-to-door time includes getting to/from stations, waiting, etc. Flying 500 miles isn't faster than driving when total time is considered.

## Common Mistakes and How to Recover

**Forgetting to include stops.** Average speed = total distance / total time. Include break time in total time.

**Using arithmetic mean for speeds.** For equal times at different speeds, arithmetic mean works. For equal distances, use harmonic mean.

**Mixing unit systems.** Don't mix km with mph or meters with hours. Convert everything to consistent units first.

**Ignoring acceleration/deceleration.** These calculations assume constant speed. Real journeys include starting, stopping, and speed changes.

**Confusing speed and velocity.** Speed is magnitude only. Velocity includes direction. For basic calculations, they're interchangeable.

## Related Topics

**Velocity.** Speed with direction specified. A vector quantity, unlike scalar speed.

**Acceleration.** Rate of change of velocity. Important for non-constant speed situations.

**Pace.** Inverse of speed, often used in running (minutes per mile or km).

**Average versus instantaneous.** Average over a journey versus speed at a specific moment.

**Relative speed.** Speed of one object relative to another, important for overtaking calculations.

## FAQs

**How do I calculate average speed with stops?**

Include all time—driving and stops—in total time. Example: 100 miles in 2 hours driving plus 30-minute stop = 100 / 2.5 = 40 mph average.

**Can I mix units?**

No—use consistent units throughout. The calculator converts between unit systems, but don't mix km with mph manually.

**Why is my average speed lower than expected?**

Because average speed includes slow segments and stops. A brief traffic jam dramatically reduces the overall average.

**Does this work for running or cycling?**

Yes. Enter distance and time to get speed, or convert to pace (min/km or min/mile) by inverting.

**How do I convert speed units?**

The calculator handles conversions. For manual conversion: mph × 1.609 = km/h; km/h × 0.621 = mph.

**What if I have multiple trip legs?**

Use "Add Leg" to enter each segment. The calculator sums total distance and time for correct average speed.

**Is this suitable for physics problems?**

Yes, for constant-speed kinematics. For acceleration problems, additional formulas apply.

**How accurate are the calculations?**

Mathematically precise for the inputs provided. Real-world accuracy depends on how well inputs match actual conditions.
