## The Core Principle: Base-60 Arithmetic

Time uses base-60 for minutes and seconds, unlike our base-10 number system. This requires special handling for carries and borrows.

**Base-60 relationships:**
- 60 seconds = 1 minute
- 60 minutes = 1 hour
- 24 hours = 1 day

**Addition with carry:**
2:45 + 1:30 = 3:75 → 4:15 (75 minutes = 1 hour 15 minutes)

**Subtraction with borrow:**
3:15 - 1:45 = 2:75 - 1:45 = 1:30 (borrow 60 minutes from hours)

**Decimal conversion:**
HH:MM → Decimal hours = Hours + (Minutes / 60)
7:30 → 7 + (30/60) = 7.5 decimal hours

**Time difference across midnight:**
If end time &lt; start time, add 24 hours to end time before subtracting.
10:00 PM to 6:00 AM = 22:00 to 30:00 = 8 hours

## How to Calculate Time Manually

**Example: Calculate work hours with lunch break**

Clock in: 8:47 AM
Clock out: 5:22 PM
Lunch: 40 minutes (unpaid)

**Step 1: Convert to 24-hour**
8:47 and 17:22

**Step 2: Calculate raw difference**
17:22 - 8:47
Minutes: 22 - 47 = -25 → borrow 60 → 82 - 47 = 35
Hours: 16 - 8 = 8 (after borrow: 17-1=16)
Raw time: 8:35

**Step 3: Subtract lunch**
8:35 - 0:40
Minutes: 35 - 40 = -5 → borrow 60 → 95 - 40 = 55
Hours: 7 (after borrow: 8-1=7)
Net time: 7:55

**Step 4: Convert to decimal**
7 + (55/60) = 7 + 0.917 = 7.92 decimal hours

## Trade-Offs and Decisions People Underestimate

**Precision requirements.** Payroll often rounds to nearest quarter hour (0.25). Check your company's policy before rounding.

**12-hour versus 24-hour.** 24-hour format eliminates AM/PM confusion. Use it for calculations, convert for display if needed.

**Seconds inclusion.** Most timesheet calculations ignore seconds. Include them only when precision matters (sports, scientific timing).

**Rounding methods.** Round to nearest? Always up? Always down? Different policies have different fairness implications.

**Break tracking.** Some systems auto-deduct breaks; others require manual entry. Know your system's behavior.

## Common Mistakes and How to Recover

**Forgetting AM/PM.** 8:00 to 5:00 is ambiguous. Is that 9 hours or 21 hours? Use 24-hour format for clarity.

**Mixing formats.** Don't add 1.5 hours (decimal) to 2:30 (HH:MM). Convert to the same format first.

**Cross-midnight errors.** A shift ending after midnight requires special handling. The calculator manages this automatically.

**Decimal versus time confusion.** 7.5 hours = 7:30, not 7:50. The decimal represents fractions of an hour, not minutes.

**Rounding before final calculation.** Keep full precision through all calculations. Round only the final result.

## Related Topics

**Decimal time.** Expressing time as fractions of an hour (7.5 = 7 hours 30 minutes). Essential for payroll.

**24-hour time.** Military time that eliminates AM/PM ambiguity. 13:00 = 1:00 PM.

**Time zones.** When calculating across zones, convert to a common reference (UTC) first.

**Duration versus clock time.** Duration is a length of time; clock time is a specific moment. Different concepts, similar notation.

**ISO 8601.** International standard for date and time representation.

## How This Calculator Works

**Time to seconds conversion:**
```
totalSeconds = (hours × 3600) + (minutes × 60) + seconds
```

**Seconds to time conversion:**
```
hours = floor(totalSeconds / 3600)
remaining = totalSeconds % 3600
minutes = floor(remaining / 60)
seconds = remaining % 60
```

**Addition:**
```
result = time1Seconds + time2Seconds
convert result back to HH:MM:SS
```

**Subtraction:**
```
result = time1Seconds - time2Seconds
if result &lt; 0 and calculating time difference:
  add 24 hours (86400 seconds)
convert result back to HH:MM:SS
```

**Decimal conversion:**
```
decimalHours = hours + (minutes / 60) + (seconds / 3600)
```

All calculations happen locally in your browser.

## FAQs

**How do I calculate time for a night shift?**

Enter times as-is. If your shift crosses midnight (10 PM to 6 AM), the calculator automatically handles the day change.

**Can I add more than two durations?**

Yes. Use the "Add Duration" button to sum multiple time entries for weekly totals or project tracking.

**Why convert to decimal hours?**

Payroll systems typically require decimal hours (7.75) rather than HH:MM format (7:45) for wage calculations.

**Does this work for seconds?**

Yes. Enter HH:MM:SS for second-level precision in sports timing, scientific measurements, or detailed tracking.

**How do I handle AM/PM?**

The calculator accepts both 12-hour (with AM/PM) and 24-hour formats. For time differences, 24-hour is recommended to avoid errors.

**Can I multiply time by a number?**

Yes. If a task takes 1:15 and you do it 8 times, calculate 1:15 × 8 = 10:00 total.

**What if my total exceeds 24 hours?**

No problem. Results display as hours:minutes (e.g., 37:45) for totals exceeding one day. Days can be shown separately.

**Is this suitable for timesheets?**

Yes. It's designed for accurate payroll calculations including break subtraction and decimal conversion.
