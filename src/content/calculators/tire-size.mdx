## The Core Principle: Diameter and Circumference

Tyre size codes contain all information needed to calculate physical dimensions.

**Decoding a tyre code: 205/55R16**
- 205: Section width in millimeters
- 55: Aspect ratio (sidewall height as percentage of width)
- R: Radial construction
- 16: Wheel diameter in inches

**Calculating dimensions:**
Sidewall height = Width × (Aspect ratio / 100)
205 × 0.55 = 112.75mm

Overall diameter = (2 × Sidewall) + Wheel diameter
= (2 × 112.75mm) + (16 × 25.4mm)
= 225.5mm + 406.4mm = 631.9mm = 24.88 inches

**Circumference:**
Circumference = π × Diameter
= 3.14159 × 631.9mm = 1985mm

**Revolutions per mile:**
Rev/mile = 63,360 inches / Diameter (inches)
= 63,360 / 24.88 = 2,546 revolutions

## How to Calculate Tyre Dimensions

**Example: Compare 205/55R16 to 225/45R17**

**Current tyre (205/55R16):**
- Width: 205mm
- Sidewall: 205 × 0.55 = 112.75mm
- Wheel: 16" = 406.4mm
- Diameter: (2 × 112.75) + 406.4 = 631.9mm = 24.88"

**New tyre (225/45R17):**
- Width: 225mm
- Sidewall: 225 × 0.45 = 101.25mm
- Wheel: 17" = 431.8mm
- Diameter: (2 × 101.25) + 431.8 = 634.3mm = 24.98"

**Comparison:**
- Diameter difference: +0.10" (+0.4%)
- Width difference: +20mm
- Speedometer error at 70mph: reads 70, actual 70.3mph

**Verdict:** Safe upgrade—well within 3% rule.

## Scenarios People Actually Run Into

**The speedometer surprise.** After installing larger tyres, your speedometer reads slow. A 3% larger tyre means your speedo under-reads by 3%.

**The clearance crisis.** Tyres that fit on paper may rub at full lock or over bumps. Physical test-fitting is essential.

**The AWD mismatch.** All-wheel-drive vehicles are sensitive to diameter differences between axles. Mismatched tyres cause drivetrain stress.

**The load rating oversight.** Larger tyres must still meet or exceed your vehicle's load requirements. Check the load index.

**The speed rating downgrade.** Switching to winter tyres with lower speed ratings may be fine, but the vehicle placard should be updated.

---

## Trade-Offs and Decisions People Underestimate

**Grip versus fuel economy.** Wider tyres improve grip but increase rolling resistance and fuel consumption.

**Ride comfort versus handling.** Lower profile tyres (shorter sidewall) improve handling but transmit more road imperfections.

**Aesthetics versus practicality.** Larger wheels look aggressive but cost more to replace and are more susceptible to pothole damage.

**Winter performance.** Narrower tyres often perform better in snow by cutting through to pavement rather than floating on top.

**Speedometer accuracy.** Even small diameter changes affect speedometer, odometer, and trip computer readings.

---

## Common Mistakes and How to Recover

**Exceeding the 3% rule.** Tyres outside ±3% of original diameter may trigger ABS/stability warnings and require speedometer recalibration.

**Ignoring wheel width.** Tyres have recommended wheel width ranges. A 225mm tyre on a too-narrow wheel will balloon unsafely.

**Forgetting offset.** Changing wheel offset affects clearance even if tyre dimensions match. Check both tyre and wheel fitment.

**Mixing sizes on AWD.** Front-to-rear diameter differences damage AWD systems. All four tyres should be identical (or within 2/32" tread difference).

**Overlooking load capacity.** Larger doesn't always mean stronger. Verify load index meets vehicle requirements.

## Related Topics

**Wheel offset.** Distance from the wheel mounting surface to the centerline. Affects how far the tyre sits in or out.

**Load index.** Number indicating maximum load capacity per tyre. Must meet or exceed vehicle requirements.

**Speed rating.** Letter indicating maximum safe speed. Match or exceed original equipment rating.

**TPMS calibration.** After changing tyre sizes, TPMS may need recalibration to read accurate pressures.

**Speedometer recalibration.** For significant size changes, OBD2 tools or dealer visits can correct speedometer readings.

## FAQs

**What does the 3% rule mean?**

New tyres should be within ±3% of original diameter. This keeps speedometer error below ~2mph at 70mph and ensures electronic systems work correctly.

**Can I compare metric and flotation sizes?**

Yes. Enter metric as 225/45R17 and flotation as 35x12.50R18. The calculator handles both formats.

**Why is my speedometer off after new tyres?**

Larger tyres travel farther per revolution, so your speedometer under-reads. A 3% larger tyre makes your speedo read 3% slow.

**Does this account for wheel offset?**

No. This calculator compares tyre dimensions only. Wheel offset affects lateral clearance and must be evaluated separately.

**How accurate are the calculations?**

They use standard geometric formulas. Actual mounted dimensions vary slightly by brand, model, and inflation.

**Can I use this for winter tyres?**

Yes. Ensure winter tyres stay within 3% of summer/all-season tyres to avoid recalibration needs.

**What if my new size is outside 3%?**

You may need speedometer recalibration, should check for ABS warnings, and must verify physical clearance with test fitting.

**Do I need to match the spare tyre?**

Full-size spares should match your new tyres, especially on AWD vehicles. Temporary spares are designed for short distances only.
