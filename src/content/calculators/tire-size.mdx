## Introduction
Our **tyre size calculator** compares the physical dimensions of two tyre sizes to help you choose upgrades that fit safely and function correctly. By decoding sidewall markings like `225/45R17`, it calculates overall diameter, circumference, and revolutions per mile—so you can predict speedometer error, gearing changes, and clearance issues before you buy.

### How It Works
1. **Decodes tyre codes** (metric and flotation sizes)
2. **Calculates key dimensions**:
   - Overall diameter (inches/mm)
   - Section width
   - Circumference
   - Revolutions per mile/km
3. **Compares two sizes** to show:
   - Absolute and percentage difference in diameter
   - Speedometer error at 60/70 mph
   - Clearance implications

### How to Use
1. Enter your **current tyre size** (e.g., `205/55R16`)
2. Enter your **proposed new size** (e.g., `225/45R17`)
3. The tool shows:
   - Dimensions for both tyres
   - Diameter difference (%)
   - Speedometer error
   - Fitment recommendation (within or outside 3% rule)

Ideal for planning performance upgrades, winter tyres, or off-road conversions.

<TabPanel title="FAQ">
### What does the 3% rule mean?
It means your new tyre’s **overall diameter** should be within **±3%** of the original. This keeps speedometer error below ~2 mph at 70 mph and ensures ABS/traction control systems work correctly.

### Can I compare metric and flotation sizes?
Yes. Enter metric sizes as `225/45R17` and flotation sizes as `35x12.50R18`. The calculator handles both formats.

### Why is my speedometer off after new tyres?
Larger tyres travel farther per revolution, so your car’s computer **under-reads** speed. A 3% larger tyre makes your speedo read **3% slower** than actual speed.

### Does this account for wheel offset?
No. This calculator only compares **tyre dimensions**. Wheel offset affects lateral clearance and must be evaluated separately.

### How accurate are the calculations?
They use standard geometric formulas. Actual mounted dimensions may vary slightly by brand, model, and inflation—but the estimate is accurate enough for fitment planning.

### Can I use this for winter tyres?
Yes. Just ensure the overall diameter stays within 3% of your summer or all-season tyres to avoid recalibration.

### What if my new size is outside the 3% rule?
You’ll likely need to:
- Recalibrate your speedometer (via OBD2 tool or dealer)
- Check for ABS/stability control warnings
- Verify physical clearance with a test fit

### Do I need to change my spare tyre?
If your spare is a **temporary "donut"**, it’s designed for short distances only and doesn’t need to match. If it’s a **full-size spare**, it should match your new tyres to avoid drivetrain stress (especially in AWD vehicles).
