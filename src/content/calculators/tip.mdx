## The Core Principle: Percentage of Bill

Tipping calculates a percentage of the bill as a gratuity for service.

**Basic tip formula:**
Tip = Bill Amount × (Tip Percentage / 100)

**Total with tip:**
Total = Bill Amount + Tip

**Per-person split:**
Per Person = Total / Number of People

**Tip on pre-tax versus post-tax:**
Pre-tax tipping: Calculate tip on subtotal before tax is added.
Post-tax tipping: Calculate tip on total including tax.

Pre-tax is considered proper etiquette since tax goes to the government, not the server. However, the difference is typically small.

**Example:**
$80 meal with 8% tax = $86.40 total
15% tip on pre-tax: $80 × 0.15 = $12.00
15% tip on post-tax: $86.40 × 0.15 = $12.96
Difference: $0.96

## How to Calculate Tips Manually

**Mental math shortcuts:**

**10%:** Move the decimal point left one place.
$85.00 → $8.50

**20%:** Calculate 10%, then double it.
$85.00 → $8.50 → $17.00

**15%:** Calculate 10%, then add half of that.
$85.00 → $8.50 + $4.25 = $12.75

**18%:** Calculate 20%, then subtract 10% of that.
$85.00 → $17.00 - $1.70 = $15.30

**Full calculation example:**
Bill: £80 (pre-tax)
VAT: 20% = £16
Tip: 15% on pre-tax = £12
Total: £80 + £16 + £12 = £108
Split 4 ways: £108 / 4 = £27 per person

## Scenarios People Actually Run Into

**The automatic service charge.** Many UK restaurants add 12.5% service. Check your bill before adding more.

**The group size threshold.** Restaurants often add automatic gratuity for parties of 6-8 or more. Verify before tipping again.

**The poor service dilemma.** Reducing or eliminating tip for genuinely poor service is socially acceptable. 10% signals dissatisfaction while still compensating.

**The takeout question.** Tipping on takeout is becoming more common, but typically at lower rates (10-15%) than dine-in.

**The international difference.** US servers depend on tips (15-20% expected). UK service charges are common. Japan has no tipping culture.

## Trade-Offs and Decisions People Underestimate

**Pre-tax versus post-tax.** The "correct" base is debated. Pre-tax is technically proper; post-tax is simpler. The difference is usually small.

**Service quality adjustment.** Exceptional service deserves 20%+. Standard service warrants 15-18%. Poor service may justify 10% or less.

**Rounding for convenience.** Rounding the tip or total to a whole number simplifies cash payments and mental math.

**Itemized versus equal splits.** Equal splits are easy but may be unfair when orders vary significantly. Itemized splits are fairer but more complex.

**Counter service versus table service.** Full table service traditionally warrants higher tips than counter service.

## Common Mistakes and How to Recover

**Double-tipping.** Check for automatic service charges before adding your own tip. Many group bills include gratuity.

**Tipping on tax.** Tax goes to the government. Tip on the pre-tax subtotal for proper etiquette.

**Forgetting tip in budget.** A $100 dinner becomes $115-120 with tip. Factor gratuity into spending plans.

**Awkward splits.** Use the calculator to determine exact per-person amounts rather than guessing and creating imbalances.

**Undertipping in the US.** 15% is minimum for acceptable service; 20% is standard for good service. US servers rely on tips for income.

## How This Calculator Works

**Tip calculation:**
```
if tipOnPreTax:
  tipBase = subtotal
else:
  tipBase = subtotal + tax

tipAmount = tipBase × (tipPercentage / 100)
```

**Total calculation:**
```
totalBill = subtotal + tax + tipAmount
```

**Split calculation:**
```
perPerson = totalBill / numberOfPeople
```

**Rounding options:**
```
roundedTip = round(tipAmount) // nearest whole
roundedTotal = round(totalBill) // nearest whole
roundedPer = ceiling(perPerson) // round up for splits
```

**Percentage presets:**
```
10% - minimum for poor service
15% - standard for acceptable service
18% - standard for good service
20% - generous, excellent service
25% - exceptional service or special occasions
```

All calculations happen locally in your browser.

## FAQs

**Should I tip on tax?**

Technically, tip on pre-tax subtotal. Tax goes to government, not server. However, the difference is typically small.

**How do I avoid double-tipping?**

Check your receipt for automatic service charge (common for groups of 6+). If present, additional tip is optional.

**Can I split the bill unevenly?**

The basic calculator splits equally. For itemized splits, calculate each person's share first, then apply same tip percentage.

**What's standard in the UK?**

10-15% if no service charge. Many restaurants include 12.5% service—check your bill.

**Does this work for delivery?**

Yes. Enter delivery total and apply 10-15% for standard service, more for bad weather or large orders.

**Can I round the tip?**

Yes. Use rounding options to round tip or total to nearest whole number for cash payments.

**Is tipping mandatory in the US?**

Legally no, but socially yes. 15-20% is expected for table service. Servers depend on tips as significant portion of income.

**How do I tip without a calculator?**

10%: move decimal left. 20%: double that. 15%: average of 10% and 20%. These mental shortcuts work quickly.
