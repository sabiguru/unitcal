### How It Works
The calculator uses the Fermi estimation framework:
1. **Aircraft Volume**: Uses Boeing 747-400 cabin and cargo hold volumes from technical specifications.
2. **Golf Ball Volume**: Based on the official USGA diameter of 4.27 cm.
3. **Packing Efficiency**: Applies a 64% random close packing factor for spheres.
4. **Usable Space**: Accounts for seats, galleys, and structure by assuming only 50–60% of cabin volume is fillable.

The formula is:  
**Total Golf Balls = (Usable Volume) / (Golf Ball Volume / Packing Efficiency)**

### What is a Fermi problem?
A Fermi problem is an estimation challenge that seems impossible but can be solved by breaking it into smaller parts and using logical assumptions. It’s named after physicist Enrico Fermi and is used to test structured thinking.

### Why can’t spheres fill 100% of the space?
Spheres leave gaps when packed. The maximum random packing density is **64%**—meaning 36% of the volume is empty space. Ignoring this overestimates by ~50%.

### Is this estimate realistic?
It’s **order-of-magnitude accurate** (within 2–3x of reality). You couldn’t actually load 9 million golf balls due to access and structure, but the math is sound for an idealised scenario.

### Can I use this for other objects?
Yes! Change the diameter to estimate tennis balls (6.7 cm), ping pong balls (4.0 cm), or marbles (1.5 cm). The calculator handles any spherical object.

### Why is the cabin only 55% usable?
The cabin contains seats, overhead bins, galleys, and lavatories—solid structures that can’t be filled with golf balls. 55% is a realistic estimate of empty space.

### Does the calculator include the wings or fuel tanks?
No. Only **pressurised cabin and cargo holds** are included, as those are the only accessible volumes. Wings and fuel tanks are structural and off-limits.

### What’s the point if it’s not exact?
The goal is to **demonstrate problem-solving**, not precision. In interviews or real-world planning, a rough estimate that’s logically derived is far more valuable than guessing.
