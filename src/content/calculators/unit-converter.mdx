## The Core Principle: Conversion Factors

Unit conversion multiplies or divides by defined ratios between measurement systems.

**Fundamental conversion factors:**

**Length:**
- 1 inch = 2.54 cm (exact, by definition)
- 1 foot = 12 inches = 30.48 cm
- 1 mile = 5,280 feet = 1.609344 km
- 1 meter = 100 cm = 1,000 mm

**Weight/Mass:**
- 1 pound = 453.592 grams
- 1 kilogram = 2.20462 pounds
- 1 ounce = 28.3495 grams
- 1 stone = 14 pounds = 6.35 kg

**Volume:**
- 1 US gallon = 3.78541 liters
- 1 UK gallon = 4.54609 liters
- 1 liter = 1,000 ml = 0.001 m³
- 1 cup (US) = 236.588 ml

**Temperature:**
- °F = (°C × 9/5) + 32
- °C = (°F - 32) × 5/9
- K = °C + 273.15

## How to Convert Units Manually

**Length: Convert 5 miles to kilometers**
5 miles × 1.609 km/mile = 8.045 km

**Weight: Convert 150 pounds to kilograms**
150 lb × 0.4536 kg/lb = 68.04 kg

**Volume: Convert 2 liters to US cups**
2 L × (1000 ml/L) × (1 cup/236.588 ml) = 8.45 cups

**Temperature: Convert 98.6°F to Celsius**
(98.6 - 32) × 5/9 = 66.6 × 5/9 = 37°C

**Speed: Convert 100 km/h to mph**
100 km/h ÷ 1.609 = 62.14 mph

**Area: Convert 1 acre to square meters**
1 acre × 4,046.86 m²/acre = 4,046.86 m²

## Scenarios People Actually Run Into

**The US versus UK gallon confusion.** A US gallon is 3.79 liters; a UK gallon is 4.55 liters. Fuel economy figures differ significantly.

**The cooking cup ambiguity.** US cups (236ml) differ from metric cups (250ml) and UK cups (284ml). Baking precision requires knowing which system.

**The stone weight mystery.** Common in UK for body weight (1 stone = 14 lb = 6.35 kg). Rarely used elsewhere.

**The nautical mile difference.** Nautical miles (1.852 km) differ from statute miles (1.609 km). Critical for aviation and maritime.

**The fluid ounce confusion.** US fluid ounce (29.6 ml) differs from UK fluid ounce (28.4 ml).

## Trade-Offs and Decisions People Underestimate

**Precision versus practicality.** For everyday use, 1 inch ≈ 2.5 cm is close enough. For engineering, use exact factors.

**Weight versus mass.** Pounds and kilograms technically measure different things (force versus mass), but are interchangeable at Earth's surface.

**Dry versus liquid measures.** Volume measures for liquids (cups, liters) may not accurately measure dry ingredients. Use weight for precision.

**Regional standards.** The same unit name may mean different quantities in different countries (gallons, cups, tons).

**Compound conversions.** Speed (distance/time), density (mass/volume), and pressure require converting both component units.

## Common Mistakes and How to Recover

**US versus UK unit confusion.** Always specify which gallon, cup, or ton you mean. The differences are significant.

**Forgetting squared/cubed factors.** Area uses length² (1 m² = 10,000 cm²). Volume uses length³ (1 m³ = 1,000,000 cm³).

**Temperature formula errors.** Remember the formula: °F = °C × 1.8 + 32. The order of operations matters.

**Using approximate factors for precision work.** 1 inch = 2.54 cm exactly. Using 2.5 introduces 1.6% error.

**Mixing systems mid-calculation.** Convert all values to one system first, then calculate, then convert back if needed.

## How This Converter Works

**Direct conversion:**
```
result = inputValue × conversionFactor
```

**Two-step conversion (via base unit):**
```
baseValue = inputValue × toBaseFactor
result = baseValue × fromBaseFactor
```

**Temperature (special formulas):**
```
// Celsius to Fahrenheit
fahrenheit = celsius × 1.8 + 32

// Fahrenheit to Celsius
celsius = (fahrenheit - 32) / 1.8

// Celsius to Kelvin
kelvin = celsius + 273.15
```

**Area and volume (squared/cubed factors):**
```
// Area: square the linear factor
1 ft² = (0.3048)² m² = 0.0929 m²

// Volume: cube the linear factor
1 ft³ = (0.3048)³ m³ = 0.0283 m³
```

All conversions use internationally standardized factors and happen locally in your browser.

**Can I convert area and volume?**

**What if my unit isn't listed?**

The converter covers common units. For rare units, use dimensional analysis: multiply and divide by known conversion factors.

**Is this suitable for scientific work?**
