## What is a BMI Calculator?

A **BMI calculator** is a simple yet valuable health screening tool that estimates your Body Mass Index. Your BMI is a number calculated from your weight and height that provides a reliable indicator of body fatness for most people. This **body mass index calculator** is used by healthcare professionals and individuals worldwide to quickly assess whether a person is underweight, normal weight, overweight, or obese. It's an excellent first step towards understanding your weight-related health risks and starting a conversation about your wellbeing.

## The Formula Explained Simply

The **BMI calculation** is based on a straightforward mathematical formula that relates your weight to your height squared. This accounts for the fact that taller people tend to weigh more than shorter people.

**Metric Formula:**
`BMI = weight (kg) / [height (m)]²`

**Imperial Formula:**
`BMI = [weight (lb) / [height (in)]²] x 703`

The number 703 is a conversion factor that makes the imperial formula's result equivalent to the metric formula's result. Our calculator automatically handles this maths for you, ensuring an accurate result regardless of the units you use.

## A Step-by-Step Example

Let's walk through a typical example to see how the formula works.

**Scenario:** Sarah wants to calculate her BMI. She weighs 68 kg and is 1.65 metres tall.

1.  **Square the height:** 1.65 m x 1.65 m = 2.7225 m².
2.  **Divide the weight by the squared height:** 68 kg / 2.7225 m² ≈ 24.98.

**Result:** Sarah's BMI is approximately **25.0**, which is at the threshold between the Normal weight and Overweight categories. This suggests she is in a healthy range but could potentially benefit from maintaining her current weight.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### How accurate is the BMI calculator?
Our calculator is highly accurate as it uses the standard formulas endorsed by health organisations like the WHO. However, the BMI number itself has limitations. It does not distinguish between weight from muscle and weight from fat. Therefore, it may be less accurate for very muscular individuals, pregnant women, or the elderly.

### What do the BMI categories mean?
The categories are based on extensive research linking BMI to health risks:
- **Underweight (Below 18.5):** May indicate malnutrition or other health issues.
- **Normal weight (18.5 – 24.9):** Associated with the lowest risk of weight-related health problems.
- **Overweight (25.0 – 29.9):** May increase the risk of conditions like heart disease and type 2 diabetes.
- **Obese (30.0 and above):** Significantly increases the risk of serious health conditions.

### Can I use BMI if I am an athlete?
BMI may not be accurate for athletes. Because muscle is denser than fat, athletes with high muscle mass may have a BMI that classifies them as overweight even though they have a low body fat percentage. In such cases, a **body fat calculator** may be more appropriate.

### Is BMI the same for children and adults?
No. While the formula is the same, the interpretation is different for children and teens. Their BMI is compared to growth charts for others of the same age and sex, resulting in a percentile. Our calculator is designed for adults aged 20 and over.
