## Introduction
Our **stoichiometry calculator** automates the core steps of chemical reaction calculations: balancing equations, converting between mass and moles, identifying the **limiting reagent**, and computing **theoretical and percent yields**. It’s ideal for GCSE, A-Level, and undergraduate chemistry students who need to verify homework or understand reaction stoichiometry quickly.

### How It Works
1. **Balances** your chemical equation using standard algorithms.
2. **Converts** input masses to moles using up-to-date atomic weights.
3. **Applies mole ratios** from the balanced equation.
4. **Identifies the limiting reagent** by comparing product yields.
5. **Calculates theoretical yield** and **percent yield** (if actual yield is provided).

### How to Use
1. Enter your unbalanced chemical equation (e.g., `Fe + O2 -> Fe2O3`)
2. Input the **mass (or moles)** of each reactant
3. (Optional) Enter the **actual yield** to compute percent yield
4. The tool shows:
   - Balanced equation
   - Moles of each reactant
   - Limiting reagent
   - Theoretical yield (in grams and moles)
   - Percent yield (if applicable)

This calculator supports hydrates (e.g., `CuSO4·5H2O`) and common polyatomic ions.

## Step-by-Step Example
**Scenario**: You react **10.0 g of hydrogen (H₂)** with **64.0 g of oxygen (O₂)** to form water. What is the theoretical yield?

**Steps**:
1. Enter equation: `H2 + O2 -> H2O`
2. Input masses: H₂ = `10.0 g`, O₂ = `64.0 g`
3. The calculator:
   - Balances to: `2H₂ + O₂ → 2H₂O`
   - Converts to moles: H₂ = 4.96 mol, O₂ = 2.00 mol
   - Calculates H₂O from H₂: 4.96 mol  
     H₂O from O₂: 4.00 mol → **O₂ is limiting**
   - Theoretical yield = `4.00 mol × 18.02 g/mol = 72.1 g`

✅ **Result**: **72.1 g of H₂O** (O₂ limits the reaction)

<TabPanel title="FAQ">
### Do I need to balance the equation myself?
No. The calculator balances it automatically. Just enter the reactants and products.

### Can I use this for ionic equations?
Yes, as long as the equation is written with correct formulas (e.g., `AgNO3 + NaCl -> AgCl + NaNO3`).

### How does it handle hydrates?
Enter them with a dot, like `CuSO4·5H2O`. The calculator includes water in molar mass.

### What if I only have one reactant?
The calculator assumes the other reactants are in excess and calculates product based on the given reactant.

### Does it work with gas volumes?
Not directly. Convert gas volume to moles first (using 24.0 L/mol at RTP for UK exams), then input moles.

### Why does it show "limiting reagent" even with one reactant?
It only shows this when **two or more reactants** are provided. With one, it assumes excess others.

### Can I calculate percent yield?
Yes. Enter the **actual yield** (from your experiment), and it will compute:  
`% Yield = (Actual / Theoretical) × 100%`

### Are atomic masses up to date?
Yes. The calculator uses IUPAC 2021 standard atomic weights.
