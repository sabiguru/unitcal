Our **fitness calculator** helps you estimate key performance metrics—**one-rep max (1RM)**, **running pace**, **VO₂ max**, and **calorie burn**—using scientifically validated formulas. Whether you’re planning strength workouts, pacing a race, tracking cardiovascular fitness, or managing energy balance, this tool provides actionable data to optimise your training. By inputting your recent performance (e.g., weight lifted for reps, run time/distance), you’ll get precise estimates to set goals, avoid plateaus, and measure progress objectively.

### How It Works
The calculator uses industry-standard formulas:
- **1RM**: Epley and Brzycki methods for safe strength estimation
- **Pace**: Converts time/distance into min/mile or min/km splits
- **VO₂ Max**: Field-test formulas (e.g., 1.5-mile run) for aerobic capacity
- **Calories**: MET-based calculation using weight, activity, and duration

### Step-by-Step Example
**Scenario**: Estimating 1RM for bench press.

**Inputs**:
- Weight: 75 kg
- Reps: 6

**Calculation**:
1. **Epley**: `75 × (1 + 6/30) = 75 × 1.2 = 90 kg`  
2. **Brzycki**: `75 × 36 / (37 – 6) = 2,700 / 31 ≈ 87.1 kg`  

**Result**: Use **87 kg** as your estimated 1RM (Brzycki is more conservative).  
- **Strength training** (85% 1RM): `0.85 × 87 ≈ 74 kg` for 3–5 reps  
- **Hypertrophy** (75% 1RM): `0.75 × 87 ≈ 65 kg` for 8–12 reps  

This ensures progressive overload without guessing weights.

### Which 1RM formula is best?
**Brzycki** is more accurate for 6–12 reps; **Epley** works well for 1–5 reps. For safety, use Brzycki—it’s more conservative and reduces injury risk from overestimation.

### How do I use pace for race planning?
Calculate your goal pace (e.g., 5:00/km for a 50-min 10K), then practice running at that pace in training. Use a GPS watch to maintain even splits—avoid starting too fast.

### Is VO₂ max estimation accurate?
Field tests are 85–90% accurate for tracking trends. They won’t match lab results but reliably show improvement over time. Test under consistent conditions (same course, time of day).

### Why do calorie estimates vary?
MET values are population averages. Your actual burn depends on fitness level, efficiency, terrain, and environment. Use estimates as a baseline—adjust based on real-world results.

### Can I calculate pace for intervals?
Yes. Enter total distance and time for your interval set. For example, 5 × 1 km in 4:30/km → total time = 22:30 for 5 km.

### How often should I re-test 1RM?
Every 6–8 weeks. Frequent testing increases injury risk and doesn’t reflect true strength gains, which take time to develop.

### Does this work for cycling or swimming?
Yes. For cycling, use power (watts) or speed to estimate effort. For swimming, input distance/time to get pace (min/100m). Calorie estimates use activity-specific METs.

### Are these formulas suitable for beginners?
Yes. Beginners benefit most from structure—these calculations prevent common mistakes like lifting too heavy or pacing races poorly. Start with conservative estimates and build gradually.
