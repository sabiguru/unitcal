Our **EV range calculator** helps you estimate your electric vehicle’s real-world driving range by accounting for your **usable battery capacity**, **energy consumption**, and key variables like **speed, temperature, HVAC use, and terrain**. Unlike manufacturer estimates, this tool provides a personalized range prediction that reflects your actual driving conditions—eliminating range anxiety and enabling confident trip planning.

### How It Works
The calculator uses the fundamental energy relationship:  
**`Range = (Usable Battery Capacity) / (Adjusted Energy Consumption)`**

It starts with your baseline consumption (kWh/100 km or Wh/mi), then applies multipliers for:
- **Speed**: Higher speeds increase consumption exponentially
- **Temperature**: Cold weather reduces battery efficiency and adds HVAC load
- **HVAC**: Heating and cooling draw constant power
- **Terrain**: Hills increase energy consumption
- **Payload**: Extra weight requires more energy

The result is a realistic range estimate with a recommended **10–20% safety buffer** for unexpected conditions.

### Key Inputs
- **Usable Battery Capacity**: Your EV’s available kWh (not total capacity)
- **Baseline Consumption**: Your typical energy use (from trip computer)
- **Driving Conditions**: Select speed, temperature, HVAC, and terrain
- **Safety Buffer**: Choose 10%, 15%, or 20% reserve

The tool instantly shows:
- Adjusted energy consumption
- Realistic driving range

- Charging stop recommendations for long trips

### Step-by-Step Example
**Scenario**: Highway driving in cold weather.

**Inputs**:
- Usable Battery: 75 kWh

- Highway Adjustment: +12% (28 kWh/100 mi)

**Calculation**:
1. Adjusted Consumption: `28 × 1.15 × 1.08 = **34.7 kWh/100 mi**`
2. Raw Range: `(75 / 34.7) × 100 = **216 miles**`
3. With 15% Buffer: `216 × 0.85 = **184 miles**`

**Result**: Plan your trip with **184 miles** of reliable range, not the EPA’s 300-mile estimate.

### What is usable battery capacity?
Usable capacity is the energy available for driving—typically **5–10% less** than the total battery capacity. Manufacturers reserve this buffer to protect battery health. Always use usable capacity for accurate calculations.

### How do I find my baseline consumption?
Check your EV’s **trip computer** or **energy screen**. Most cars show long-term average consumption in kWh/100 km or Wh/mi. Reset the trip meter after a full charge for the most accurate reading.

### Why is my real range less than the EPA estimate?
EPA tests use ideal lab conditions: moderate temperatures, no HVAC, gentle acceleration, and fixed speed profiles. Real-world driving with highway speeds, temperature extremes, and aggressive driving reduces range by **20–40%**.

### How much does cold weather affect range?
In temperatures below **0°C (32°F)**, range can drop by **30–40%** due to reduced battery efficiency and energy used for cabin/battery heating.

### Does highway driving reduce range?
Yes. Highway driving at **70+ mph** can increase energy consumption by **25–30%** compared to city driving due to aerodynamic drag.

### What is a good safety buffer?
Plan to arrive with **10–20% State of Charge**. This accounts for unexpected detours, traffic, headwinds, or charging station issues.

### Can I use this for trip planning?
Yes. The calculator shows your realistic range, helping you identify where you’ll need charging stops. For detailed routing, use it alongside apps like A Better Routeplanner (ABRP).

### How accurate is this calculator?
It’s highly accurate for **personalized estimates** when you input your actual consumption data. However, individual results may vary based on driving style, road conditions, and vehicle-specific factors.
