Our **equation solver** helps you find solutions to linear, quadratic, and systems of equations with clear, detailed steps. Whether you’re checking homework, studying for an exam, or solving real-world problems, this tool breaks down the solution process using methods like **factoring**, the **quadratic formula**, and **substitution**—so you understand not just the answer, but how to get there.

### How It Works
The solver uses standard algebraic techniques:
- **Linear equations**: Isolates the variable using inverse operations.
- **Quadratic equations**: Applies factoring, completing the square, or the quadratic formula based on the equation’s structure.
- **Systems of equations**: Uses substitution or elimination to find the intersection point.

For quadratics, it calculates the **discriminant** (`b² – 4ac`) to determine the number and type of solutions (real or complex).

### Key Features
- **Step-by-step solutions**: See every algebraic step, not just the final answer.
- **Multiple methods**: Choose your preferred solving technique or let the solver pick the most efficient one.
- **Complex number support**: Handles equations with imaginary solutions.
- **Fractional precision**: Keeps answers exact using fractions, not decimals.
- **Verification**: Automatically checks solutions in the original equation.

### Step-by-Step Example
**Scenario**: Solve the quadratic equation `2x² + 4x – 6 = 0`.

**Inputs**:
- a = 2
- b = 4
- c = –6

**Solution Process**:
1. **Calculate discriminant**: `D = b² – 4ac = 16 – 4(2)(–6) = 16 + 48 = 64`
2. **Apply quadratic formula**:  
   `x = [–4 ± √64] / (2×2) = [–4 ± 8] / 4`
3. **Find roots**:  
   `x₁ = (–4 + 8)/4 = 1`  
   `x₂ = (–4 – 8)/4 = –3`

**Result**: The solutions are **x = 1** and **x = –3**.  
Both satisfy the original equation:  
- `2(1)² + 4(1) – 6 = 0` ✓  
- `2(–3)² + 4(–3) – 6 = 0` ✓

### What types of equations can this solver handle?
It solves **linear equations** (`ax + b = 0`), **quadratic equations** (`ax² + bx + c = 0`), and **systems of two linear equations**. It supports real and complex solutions.

### How does it choose a solving method?
For quadratics, it first attempts **factoring**. If that fails, it uses the **quadratic formula**. For systems, it selects **substitution** or **elimination** based on coefficient simplicity.

### What is the discriminant and why does it matter?
The **discriminant** (`b² – 4ac`) tells you how many real solutions a quadratic has:
- **Positive**: Two real solutions
- **Zero**: One real solution
- **Negative**: Two complex solutions

### Can it solve equations with fractions?
Yes. Enter coefficients as fractions (e.g., `1/2`), and the solver will maintain exact fractional form throughout the solution.

### Why are there two solutions for a quadratic?
A quadratic equation represents a parabola, which can cross the x-axis at **0, 1, or 2 points**. Each crossing point is a real solution.

### Does it show work for systems of equations?
Yes. It displays every step of substitution or elimination, including how variables are isolated and back-substituted.

### Can I use this for homework?
Absolutely. It’s designed to help you **learn**, not just get answers. The step-by-step breakdown teaches you the method so you can solve similar problems on your own.

### What if my equation has no solution?
The solver will indicate **“no real solution”** (for quadratics with negative discriminant) or **“no solution”** (for inconsistent systems like parallel lines).
