## Step-by-Step: Scheduling a Call from London to Sydney
**Scenario**: You’re in London and want to call a colleague in Sydney at a reasonable hour. You propose **2:00 PM London time** on July 15.

1. **Enter Source**:  
   - Time: **2:00 PM**

2. **Enter Target**:  
   - Zone: **Sydney (AEST/AEDT)**

3. **Calculate**:  
   - London 2:00 PM BST = **1:00 AM next day in Sydney**  
   - *Too late!*

4. **Adjust**: Try **9:00 AM London time** → **7:00 PM Sydney** (acceptable).

<TabPanel title="FAQ">
### Does this converter account for Daylight Saving Time?
Yes. It uses the **IANA Time Zone Database** to apply correct DST rules based on your selected date. Always enter the exact date of your event.

### What is UTC, and why is it used?
**UTC (Coordinated Universal Time)** is the global time standard. All time zones are offsets from UTC. Using UTC eliminates ambiguity in international coordination.

### Why does the date sometimes change?
Crossing the **International Date Line** (near 180° longitude) changes the calendar day. The converter handles this automatically—e.g., a flight from LA to Tokyo may land “tomorrow.”

### Can I compare more than two time zones?
Yes! Use the “Add Zone” button to compare up to 6 time zones simultaneously—ideal for team meetings across continents.

### How do I know if a location observes DST?
The converter shows the **current offset** (e.g., “London: UTC+1 (BST)”) and adjusts automatically. You don’t need to know DST rules—just select the city and date.

### What if my country recently changed its time zone?
Our database is updated regularly. For critical plans, verify with official government sources close to your event date.

### Can I use this for historical dates?
Yes, but note that DST rules have changed over time. The converter uses the best available historical data.

### Why are some time zones 30 or 45 minutes off?
Some regions (e.g., India, Nepal) use fractional offsets for geographical or historical reasons. The converter handles these precisely.

### Is this suitable for flight planning?
Yes—enter your departure time and zone, then convert to your destination zone to estimate arrival time (add flight duration manually).

### How do I schedule a fair meeting for a global team?
1. Use the **multi-zone view** to see everyone’s local time.  
2. Aim for **overlap in business hours** (e.g., 9 AM–5 PM local).  
3. **Rotate times** so the same people aren’t always inconvenienced.
