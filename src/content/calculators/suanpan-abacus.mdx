## How to Use This Calculator - Suanpan Abacus
Clear the abacus by moving all beads away from the center beam.

Click beads to move them toward or away from the beam:
- Upper beads (2 per rod): each worth 5 when touching beam
- Lower beads (5 per rod): each worth 1 when touching beam

Read the number from left to right. Each rod represents a place value.

Use the Clear button to reset all rods to zero.

The digital display shows the current value for verification.

## The Core Principle: The 2:5 Bead System

The suanpan is a 2:5 abacus—two upper beads (heaven beads) worth 5 each, and five lower beads (earth beads) worth 1 each.

**Maximum value per rod:**
2 × 5 + 5 × 1 = 15

This exceeds 9 (needed for decimal), providing flexibility for intermediate calculations.

**Reading a rod:**
- Each upper bead touching beam: add 5
- Each lower bead touching beam: add 1
- Example: 1 upper + 3 lower = 5 + 3 = 8

**Place value:**
Rods represent units, tens, hundreds from right to left. The decimal point can be placed mentally at any position.

**Complement rules:**
- Complement to 5: pairs that sum to 5 (1&4, 2&3)
- Complement to 10: pairs that sum to 10 (1&9, 2&8, 3&7, 4&6)

These rules enable efficient addition and subtraction without counting beads.

## How to Calculate on a Suanpan

**Addition example: 348 + 576**

**Step 1: Set 348**
- Hundreds: 3 lower beads up (3)
- Tens: 4 lower beads up (4)
- Units: 1 upper + 3 lower (5 + 3 = 8)

**Step 2: Add 6 to units**
- Units: 8 + 6 = 14 → need to carry
- Using complement: +6 = +10 - 4
- Subtract 4 from units: 8 - 4 = 4 (move 1 upper up, move 1 lower up)
- Carry 1 to tens

**Step 3: Add 7 to tens (including carry)**
- Tens: 4 + 1 (carry) + 7 = 12 → need to carry
- Using complement: +7 = +10 - 3
- Current tens: 5, subtract 3: 5 - 3 = 2
- Carry 1 to hundreds

**Step 4: Add 5 to hundreds (including carry)**
- Hundreds: 3 + 1 (carry) + 5 = 9
- Show 9: 1 upper + 4 lower beads

**Result: 924**

**Subtraction example: 724 - 358**

**Step 1: Set 724**
**Step 2: Subtract 8 from units**
- Units: 4 - 8 → can't directly
- Using complement: -8 = -10 + 2
- Borrow from tens (becomes 1)
- Add 2 to units: 4 + 2 = 6

**Step 3: Subtract 5 from tens**
- Tens: 1 - 5 → can't directly
- Borrow from hundreds (becomes 6)
- Add 5 to tens: 1 + 10 - 5 = 6

**Step 4: Subtract 3 from hundreds**
- Hundreds: 6 - 3 = 3

**Result: 366**

## Scenarios People Actually Run Into

**The extra beads question.** Why 2+5 when you only need 9? The extras allow intermediate values during carries and borrows without immediate resolution.

**The direction confusion.** Beads touching the beam are "active." Moving toward beam adds value; moving away subtracts.

**The complement learning curve.** Complements feel awkward at first but are essential for speed. Drill them until automatic.

**The decimal placement.** For decimals, mentally assign any rod as the units place. Rods to the right become tenths, hundredths, etc.

**The soroban comparison.** Japanese soroban (1:4) is more streamlined; Chinese suanpan (2:5) offers more flexibility. Both use the same principles.

## Trade-Offs and Decisions People Underestimate

**2:5 versus 1:4.** The suanpan's extra beads allow more intermediate states but require more physical movement. The soroban is optimized for speed.

**Physical versus mental.** Physical practice builds foundations; mental visualization (anzan) is the advanced goal.

**Speed versus accuracy.** Rushing causes errors. Build accuracy first, then gradually increase speed.

**Traditional versus modern.** The suanpan is slower than calculators for simple operations but builds skills that transfer to mental math.

**Learning investment.** Proficiency requires regular practice. Casual use provides limited benefit.

## Common Mistakes and How to Recover

**Not clearing before starting.** Leftover beads from previous calculations cause errors. Always clear first.

**Forgetting carries.** When a rod exceeds 9 (or 15), carry to the next rod. Missing carries produces wrong answers.

**Counting instead of using complements.** Counting beads one by one defeats the purpose. Learn and apply complement rules.

**Wrong complement type.** Complement to 5 and complement to 10 are different operations. Know when each applies.

**Reading right to left.** Read left to right (high to low place value), like standard number notation.

## How This Calculator Works

**Bead value calculation:**
```
for each rod:
  upperValue = count(upperBeads touching beam) × 5
  lowerValue = count(lowerBeads touching beam) × 1
  rodValue = upperValue + lowerValue

totalValue = sum(rodValue × 10^position)
```

**Setting a number:**
```
for each digit (right to left):
  if digit >= 10:
    use 2 upper beads (10) + remainder
  else if digit >= 5:
    use 1 upper bead (5) + (digit - 5) lower beads
  else:
    use digit lower beads
```

**Complement operations:**
```
// Complement to 5
if adding n when lower beads full:
  add 5 (move 1 upper down)
  subtract (5 - n) from lower beads

// Complement to 10
if adding n when rod would exceed capacity:
  add 10 (carry 1 to next rod)
  subtract (10 - n) from current rod
```

**Display synchronization:**
```
Read all rod values
Combine into decimal number
Update digital display
```

## FAQs

**How do I represent the number 7?**

Move 1 upper bead down (value 5) and 2 lower beads up (value 2). Total: 5 + 2 = 7.

**What are complements?**

Pairs summing to 5 or 10. They enable efficient calculation without counting beads individually.

**Can I do decimals?**

Yes. Choose any rod as the units place; rods to the right become tenths, hundredths, etc.

**How is the suanpan different from the soroban?**

The suanpan has 2 upper and 5 lower beads (2:5); the soroban has 1 upper and 4 lower (1:4). Suanpan allows more intermediate states.

**Do I need to know Chinese?**

No. The suanpan is a mathematical tool; bead values and operations are universal.

**Is this useful for modern calculations?**

It builds mental math skills that transfer beyond the abacus. Speed isn't the point—cognitive development is.

**How long until I'm proficient?**

Basic operations: 2-4 weeks. Advanced speed and mental visualization: months to years of regular practice.

**Can I use this for multiplication and division?**

Yes, though these operations are more complex. Master addition and subtraction first.
