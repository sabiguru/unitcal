## Introduction
Our **Western abacus calculator** is an interactive, online version of the classic 10-bead school abacus. It's designed to help children and beginners **visualise place value**, practice **counting**, and **perform addition and subtraction** with regrouping (carrying and borrowing). Each rod has 10 beads, perfectly mirroring the base-10 number system.

### Core Features
- **10 beads per rod**: All beads on a rod have the same value (1, 10, 100, etc.)
- **Place value rods**: Units, tens, hundreds, thousands
- **Regrouping support**: Visualise carrying and borrowing in real time
- **Input options**: Click beads manually or type a number to auto-display
- **Clear/reset**: Instantly return to zero

### How to Use
1. **Clear the abacus** to start from zero
2. **Build a number** by clicking beads to move them to the active area (right side)
3. **Perform addition** by moving more beads to the right
4. **Perform subtraction** by moving beads back to the left
5. **Regroup automatically**: When you have 10 beads on a rod, carry to the next; when borrowing, exchange 1 higher bead for 10 lower ones
6. **Read the result** from the digital display above

Ideal for home learning, classroom demonstrations, or building confidence in early arithmetic.


## Step-by-Step Example
**Problem**: Add **27 + 15** using the Western abacus.

**Steps**:
1. **Set 27**:
   - Click 2 beads on the **tens rod** (value = 20)
   - Click 7 beads on the **units rod** (value = 7)
2. **Add 5 to units**:
   - Click 5 more beads on the units rod → total = 12 beads
3. **Carry**:
   - The calculator automatically carries: 10 units beads are cleared, and 1 bead is added to the tens rod
   - Units now show **2**, tens show **3**
4. **Add 1 to tens**:
   - Click 1 more bead on the tens rod → tens now show **4**
5. **Final result**: **42**

✅ **Answer**: **42**

This demonstrates how the abacus makes regrouping visible and intuitive.


### How do I represent the number 8?
Move **8 beads** on the **units rod** to the active area (right side). Each bead = 1, so 8 beads = 8.

### What is regrouping?
**Regrouping** is exchanging 10 of a smaller unit for 1 of the next larger unit (e.g., 10 ones = 1 ten). It’s the foundation of carrying in addition and borrowing in subtraction.

### Can I use this for decimals?
Yes. The rod to the right of the units rod represents **tenths**, the next **hundredths**, etc. All operations work the same way.

### How is this different from a soroban?
The **Western abacus** has **10 beads per rod**, all of equal value, and is for **learning concepts**. The **soroban** (Japanese) has **1 upper + 4 lower beads** and is for **fast calculation** using complements.

### Is this suitable for older students?
Yes! It’s excellent for students who need to **reinforce place value** or struggle with regrouping. It’s also used in **special education** for its concrete, visual approach.

### Can I do multiplication?
You can model multiplication as **repeated addition** (e.g., 3 × 4 = 4 + 4 + 4). For larger products, it’s better to use a standard calculator once the concept is understood.

### How do I clear the abacus?
Click the **Clear** button, or manually move all beads back to the inactive area (left side).

### Why is the Western abacus still relevant?
Because it builds **number sense**—a deep, intuitive understanding of how numbers work. This foundation is critical for all future maths success, from fractions to algebra.
