## How to Use the pH Calculator
The **pH calculator** instantly computes **pH, pOH, [H⁺], and [OH⁻]** for strong acids/bases, weak acids/bases, and buffer solutions. Simply select your solution type, enter the concentration and Ka/Kb (if applicable), and the tool applies the correct method—whether it’s a direct log calculation, the quadratic formula, or the Henderson-Hasselbalch equation. It even shows step-by-step working, making it perfect for learning and verifying homework. For weak acids, it automatically checks if the approximation is valid or if the exact quadratic solution is needed.

### Key Formulas Used
- **Strong Acid**: `pH = -log(C)`
- **Strong Base**: `pH = 14 + log(C)`
- **Weak Acid**: Solves `Ka = x²/(C - x)` (approximation or quadratic)
- **Buffer**: `pH = pKa + log([A⁻]/[HA])`
- **Universal**: `pH + pOH = 14`, `[H⁺][OH⁻] = 1e-14`

### Best Practices for Accurate Results
1.  **Select the correct solution type**—using the wrong model gives incorrect results.
2.  **Enter Ka or Kb values accurately**—use standard tables if unsure.
3.  **For buffers**, input equilibrium concentrations of acid and conjugate base.
4.  **Assume 25°C** unless specified—the calculator uses Kw = 1.0 × 10^-14.
5.  **Verify very dilute solutions** (below 10^-6 M)—water's autoionisation may need manual adjustment.

<TabPanel title="FAQ">
### Can this calculate pH for polyprotic acids?
It handles **monoprotic** acids/bases and simple buffers. For diprotic acids (e.g., H₂SO₄, H₂CO₃), you’ll need to calculate each dissociation step separately—this tool can assist with individual steps.

### Why does it sometimes use the quadratic formula?
For weak acids where the concentration is low or Ka is high (C/Ka below 100), the standard approximation introduces significant error. The calculator automatically switches to the exact quadratic solution for accuracy.

### How do I find Ka if I know pH?
Rearrange the weak acid formula: `Ka = [H⁺]² / (C - [H⁺])`. Enter your known values, and the calculator can solve for Ka if you select the appropriate mode.

### Is temperature accounted for?
The calculator assumes **25°C** (Kw = 1.0 × 10^-¹⁴). For other temperatures, you’d need to adjust Kw manually—this is beyond the scope of a standard educational tool.

### Can I use this for very concentrated solutions?
For [H⁺] > 1 M (pH below 0) or [OH⁻] > 1 M (pH > 14), the calculator still works mathematically, but real solutions may deviate due to activity effects.

### What if my buffer has unequal volumes?
Always use **final equilibrium concentrations** in the Henderson-Hasselbalch equation. If you mix 50 mL of 0.4 M HA with 50 mL of 0.2 M A⁻, the final [HA] = 0.2 M and [A⁻] = 0.1 M.

### Does this work for basic buffers?
Yes—use the **conjugate acid** form. For an NH₃/NH₄⁺ buffer, treat NH₄⁺ as the weak acid (Ka = Kw/Kb) and NH₃ as the conjugate base.

### How precise are the results?
Results are shown to **4 significant figures** internally, with pH reported to **2 decimal places**—standard for most laboratory work.
