Our **capacitor calculator** helps you solve for capacitance, voltage, charge, energy, reactance, and time constants in both DC and AC circuits. Whether you’re designing a filter, sizing a decoupling capacitor, or calculating energy storage, this tool provides instant, accurate results using standard electrical formulas.

### How It Works
The calculator uses core capacitor equations:
- **Charge**: `Q = C × V`
- **Energy**: `E = ½ × C × V²`
- **Reactance**: `Xc = 1 / (2πfC)`
- **Time Constant**: `τ = R × C`

For **series/parallel networks**, it applies combination rules:
- **Parallel**: `C_total = C₁ + C₂ + ...`
- **Series**: `1/C_total = 1/C₁ + 1/C₂ + ...`

### Step-by-Step Example
**Scenario**: Design a high-pass filter with a **1 kHz** cutoff using a **10 kΩ** resistor.

**Inputs**:
- Filter Type: High-pass
- Cutoff Frequency: 1,000 Hz
- Resistance: 10,000 Ω

**Calculation**:
- Formula: `C = 1 / (2π × f × R)`
- `C = 1 / (2 × 3.1416 × 1000 × 10000) ≈ 15.9 nF`

**Result**: Use a **15 nF or 16 nF ceramic capacitor**. At 1 kHz, its reactance equals the resistor value (10 kΩ), creating the -3dB cutoff point.

This ensures frequencies above 1 kHz pass with minimal attenuation, while lower frequencies are blocked.

### What is capacitive reactance?
Capacitive reactance (`Xc`) is the opposition a capacitor offers to alternating current. It’s measured in ohms and calculated as `Xc = 1/(2πfC)`. Unlike resistance, it **decreases as frequency increases**.

### How do I calculate energy stored in a capacitor?
Use `E = ½ × C × V²`, where `E` is energy in joules, `C` is capacitance in farads, and `V` is voltage. For example, a 1000 µF capacitor at 12V stores **0.072 J**.

### What is an RC time constant?
The **RC time constant** (`τ = R × C`) is the time for a capacitor to charge to 63.2% of the supply voltage through a resistor. It’s used in timing circuits, filters, and delay generators.

### Can I calculate series and parallel capacitors?
Yes. For **parallel**, add capacitances directly. For **series**, use `1/C_total = 1/C₁ + 1/C₂ + ...`. The calculator handles both automatically.

### Why does my capacitor calculation not match real-world behaviour?
Real capacitors have **parasitic effects**: ESR (resistance), ESL (inductance), leakage current, and tolerance (±10–20%). Always derate voltage and check datasheets for frequency/temperature performance.

### What capacitor type should I use for high-frequency circuits?
**Ceramic capacitors** (especially C0G/NP0) are ideal for high-frequency decoupling (>1 MHz). Avoid electrolytics—they have high ESR and inductance at high frequencies.

### How do I avoid capacitor failure?
- **Never reverse polarity** on electrolytic/tantalum capacitors.  
- **Derate voltage** by 30–50%.  
- **Stay within ripple current limits** for power applications.  
- **Avoid mechanical stress** on leads or body.

### Is this calculator safe for mains voltage circuits?
No. This tool is for **low-voltage DC and signal-level AC circuits**. **Never design or troubleshoot mains-connected circuits** without professional training and safety certification.
