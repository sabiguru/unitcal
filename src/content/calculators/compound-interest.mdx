Our **compound interest calculator** projects how your savings or investments will grow over time by accounting for your initial deposit, regular contributions, interest rate, and compounding frequency. Whether you’re saving for retirement, a house, or education, this tool shows the powerful effect of compounding—and how small, consistent contributions can lead to significant long-term wealth.

### How It Works
The calculator uses the standard compound interest formula:
- **Without contributions**: `FV = P × (1 + r/n)^(n×t)`
- **With contributions**: `FV = P × (1 + r/n)^(n×t) + PMT × [((1 + r/n)^(n×t) - 1) / (r/n)]`

Where **`P`** is your starting amount, **`PMT`** is your monthly contribution, **`r`** is your annual interest rate, **`n`** is compounding frequency, and **`t`** is time in years.

### Key Inputs
- **Initial Investment**: Your starting balance.
- **Monthly Contribution**: Regular deposits (e.g., salary savings).
- **Annual Interest Rate**: Expected return (e.g., 4% for savings, 7% for investments).
- **Compounding Frequency**: Daily, monthly, quarterly, or annually.
- **Time Horizon**: Number of years you’ll invest.

The tool instantly shows:
- Future value of your investment
- Total contributions (principal + deposits)
- Total interest earned
- Year-by-year growth chart

### How to Use
1. Enter your **initial investment** (e.g., £5,000).
2. Add your **monthly contribution** (e.g., £200).
3. Input your expected **annual return** (use 4–7% for long-term investments).
4. Select **compounding frequency** (monthly is standard for most accounts).
5. Set your **time horizon** (e.g., 30 years for retirement).
6. Click **Calculate** to see your projected growth.

Use the results to adjust your savings rate, time horizon, or investment strategy to meet your financial goals.

<TabPanel title="FAQ">
### What is compound interest?
Compound interest is interest calculated on your initial principal **and** all previously accumulated interest. This creates exponential growth over time, unlike simple interest, which only applies to the original amount.

### How does compounding frequency affect growth?
More frequent compounding (e.g., daily vs. annually) leads to slightly higher returns because interest is added to your balance more often, allowing it to earn its own interest sooner.

### What is a realistic annual return?
- **Savings accounts**: 1–4% (low risk, guaranteed)
- **Government bonds**: 3–5% (low-medium risk)
- **Stock market (long-term)**: 5–7% after inflation (medium-high risk, not guaranteed)

Use conservative estimates for long-term planning.

### Can I use this for debt?
Yes—but in reverse. High-interest debt (e.g., credit cards at 24% APR) compounds **against you**, making balances grow rapidly. Always prioritise paying off high-interest debt before investing.

### Should I include inflation?
The calculator shows **nominal returns**. For real-world purchasing power, subtract expected inflation (e.g., 2–3%) from your annual return. A 6% return with 3% inflation = 3% real growth.

### Is compound interest taxable?
In the UK, interest from savings is taxable unless held in a **Cash ISA**. Investment gains are tax-free in **Stocks and Shares ISAs** or **SIPPs**. Always use tax-efficient accounts to maximise compounding.

### How accurate is this calculator?
It’s mathematically precise for **fixed-rate scenarios** (e.g., savings accounts). For investments, actual returns will vary due to market fluctuations—use it as a projection, not a guarantee.
