## What is a Budget Calculator?

A **budget calculator** is a practical financial tool that helps you create a plan for your money. It moves you from wondering where your money went to telling it where to go. By using frameworks like the popular **50/30/20 rule**, our calculator provides a clear structure to allocate your income towards your needs, wants, and financial goals. This **monthly budget planner** is designed for anyone looking to reduce financial stress, build savings, pay off debt, or simply gain a clearer understanding of their cash flow. It’s the first and most important step towards achieving financial control and freedom.

## The 50/30/20 Rule Explained Simply

The **50/30/20 rule** is a simple and effective guideline for budgeting. It suggests dividing your after-tax income into three categories:

- **50% Needs:** Essential expenses you must pay to live and work, such as rent/mortgage, utilities, groceries, basic transport, and minimum debt payments.
- **30% Wants:** Discretionary spending on things that enhance your lifestyle, like dining out, entertainment, holidays, and hobbies.
- **20% Savings & Debt:** Money dedicated to your future, including emergency savings, retirement funds, and extra payments to pay down debt faster.

This framework ensures your essentials are covered, allows for enjoyment, and makes saving a priority.

## How to Use This Calculator

Using our **budget calculator** is a straightforward process that delivers powerful insights.

1.  **Enter Your Monthly Income:** Input your take-home pay (the amount that hits your bank account after taxes and other deductions). If your income varies, use a conservative average.
2.  **List Your Expenses:** Add your regular monthly expenses. The calculator will help you categorise them as Needs, Wants, or Savings/Debt.
3.  **Analyse Your Results:** The calculator will show you how your current spending compares to the 50/30/20 targets. It highlights areas where you are over or under budget.
4.  **Create Your Plan:** Adjust your planned spending in each category to align with your financial goals. The tool lets you experiment with different scenarios instantly.


## A Step-by-Step Example

Let's see how the 50/30/20 rule works for a typical scenario.

**Scenario:** Alex has a monthly take-home pay of £2,400.

1.  **Calculate Allocations:**
    - **Needs:** 50% of £2,400 = **£1,200**
    - **Wants:** 30% of £2,400 = **£720**
    - **Savings/Debt:** 20% of £2,400 = **£480**

2.  **Alex's Current Spending:**
    - *Needs:* Rent (£800), Bills (£150), Food (£250), Transport (£100) = **£1,300**
    - *Wants:* Eating out (£200), Entertainment (£100), Shopping (£150) = **£450**
    - *Savings/Debt:* Savings (£200) = **£200**

3.  **Analysis:** Alex's Needs are over budget by £100, and Savings are £280 under target. Wants have some room.

4.  **The Plan:** Alex decides to reduce food costs by £50 per month (meal planning) and cut shopping by £100. This frees up £150.
    - **New Needs:** £1,250 (slightly over, but better)
    - **New Wants:** £350
    - **New Savings/Debt:** £350 + £150 = **£500** (Now exceeding the target!)

This simple adjustment puts Alex on a much stronger financial path.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What counts as a "Need" vs. a "Want"?
This is the most common question. A **Need** is an expense you cannot avoid without significant consequences to your health, work, or housing. A **Want** is an expense that improves your quality of life but is optional. For example, a basic grocery shop is a Need; dining out is a Want. A basic gym membership might be a Need for some (for health) and a Want for others—be honest with yourself.

### What if my essential needs are more than 50% of my income?
This is a common situation, especially in areas with high housing costs. If your Needs exceed 50%, the adjustment must come from your Wants category. The 20% for Savings/Debt is the most important category for long-term financial health, so try to protect it. You may need to temporarily use a 60/20/20 split until you can increase your income or reduce essential costs.

### How do I handle irregular expenses like car repairs or holidays?
The key is to plan for them. For irregular but predictable expenses (e.g., car insurance paid annually, holiday spending), calculate the total annual cost and divide it by 12. Save that amount each month in a "sinking fund" within your Savings/Debt category. When the bill comes, the money is already set aside.

### Should I use my pre-tax or post-tax income?
Always use your **post-tax income** (your take-home pay). This is the actual amount of money you have available to spend each month. Using your gross (pre-tax) income will create an unrealistic budget because that money is not yours to allocate.
