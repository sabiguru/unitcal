## Understanding Fraction to Percent Conversion

A percentage is simply a fraction with a denominator of 100. Converting a fraction to a percentage means expressing it as parts per hundred.

**Formula:**
Percentage = (Numerator ÷ Denominator) × 100

**Why it works:**
- Fractions represent parts of a whole
- Percentages represent parts per hundred
- Dividing gives the decimal form, multiplying by 100 gives the percentage

**Visual representation:**
- 1/4 means 1 part out of 4 equal parts
- 1/4 = 25/100 = 25% (25 parts out of 100)

## How to Convert Fractions to Percentages Manually

**Step 1:** Divide the numerator by the denominator
Example: 3/4 → 3 ÷ 4 = 0.75

**Step 2:** Multiply the result by 100
0.75 × 100 = 75

**Step 3:** Add the percent symbol
75%

**Alternative Method for Simple Fractions:**
Find an equivalent fraction with denominator 100
Example: 1/4 = ?/100
1/4 = 25/100 = 25%

**Method for complex fractions:**
7/16 → 7 ÷ 16 = 0.4375 → 0.4375 × 100 = 43.75%

## FAQs

**Can I convert improper fractions to percentages?**

Yes. Improper fractions (where numerator is greater than denominator) give percentages greater than 100%. For example, 5/4 = 1.25 = 125%.

**How do I handle repeating decimals?**

Some fractions like 1/3 produce repeating decimals (0.333...). Round to a reasonable number of decimal places, typically 33.33%.

**What if my fraction has a decimal numerator or denominator?**

This calculator handles decimal inputs. For example, 1.5/2 = 75%.

**Is 1/2 exactly 50%?**

Yes, some fractions convert to exact percentages, while others produce repeating or terminating decimals.

**Can I convert mixed numbers?**

Convert mixed numbers to improper fractions first. For example, 1 1/2 = 3/2 = 150%.

**What is the fastest way to convert common fractions?**

Memorise key conversions: 1/2 = 50%, 1/4 = 25%, 1/5 = 20%, 1/10 = 10%. Build from these using multiplication.

**How do I convert a percentage back to a fraction?**

Divide by 100, then simplify. 75% = 75/100 = 3/4.

**Why do some fractions give repeating decimals?**

Fractions with denominators containing prime factors other than 2 and 5 produce repeating decimals. 1/3 repeats because 3 is not a factor of 10.

**How do I handle very small fractions?**

The same method applies. 1/1000 = 0.001 = 0.1%.

**What precision should I use for the percentage?**

Match the precision of the original data. For casual use, 2 decimal places suffice. For scientific work, maintain appropriate significant figures.

**How do percentages relate to ratios?**

A ratio of 3:7 means 3 parts out of 10 total. As a fraction: 3/10 = 30%.

**Can fractions with zero in the numerator be converted?**

Yes. 0/n = 0 = 0% for any non-zero n.

**What about negative fractions?**

The conversion works the same way. -3/4 = -0.75 = -75%.

**How do unit fractions compare?**

Unit fractions (1/n) decrease as n increases: 1/2 (50%) is greater than 1/3 (33.33%) is greater than 1/4 (25%).

**What is the relationship between fractions and probability?**

Probability is often expressed as fractions (1/6 chance on a die) and converted to percentages (16.67%) for easier interpretation.
