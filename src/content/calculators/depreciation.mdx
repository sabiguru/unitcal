Our **depreciation calculator** helps you compute annual depreciation expense and book value using four standard methods: **straight-line**, **double declining balance (DDB)**, **sum-of-the-years’-digits (SYD)**, and **MACRS** (for US tax purposes). Whether you’re managing business accounts, studying accounting, or planning asset purchases, this tool provides a clear depreciation schedule to support financial reporting and tax compliance.

### How It Works
The calculator applies industry-standard formulas based on your inputs:
- **Straight-Line**: Even depreciation over asset life
- **DDB**: Accelerated depreciation (double the straight-line rate)
- **SYD**: Accelerated depreciation using remaining-life fractions
- **MACRS**: US tax method with IRS-defined rates

### Key Inputs
- **Asset Cost**: Purchase price (including delivery and setup)
- **Salvage Value**: Estimated resale value at end of life
- **Useful Life**: Number of years the asset will be used
- **Method**: Choose your depreciation approach
- **Start Date**: For partial-year calculations

The tool instantly generates:
- Annual depreciation expense
- End-of-year book value
- Full depreciation schedule (exportable)
- Visual chart of value decline

### How to Use
1. Enter the **asset cost** and **salvage value**.
2. Input the **useful life** in years.
3. Select your preferred **depreciation method**.
4. Click **Calculate** to see your schedule.
5. Compare methods to understand tax and accounting impacts.

For tax purposes, consult a professional—UK businesses use **capital allowances**, while US businesses use **MACRS**.

<TabPanel title="Example">
### Step-by-Step Example
**Scenario**: A company buys a £20,000 machine with a £2,000 salvage value and 5-year life.

**Straight-Line Method**:  
- Annual Depreciation: `(£20,000 – £2,000) ÷ 5 = **£3,600/year**`  
- Book Value after Year 3: `£20,000 – (3 × £3,600) = **£9,200**`

**Double Declining Balance**:  
- Rate: `2 ÷ 5 = 40%`  
- Year 1: `40% × £20,000 = **£8,000**`  
- Year 2: `40% × £12,000 = **£4,800**`  
- Year 3: `40% × £7,200 = **£2,880**`  
- Book Value after Year 3: **£4,320**

**Insight**: DDB provides larger early deductions, reducing taxable income faster.

<TabPanel title="FAQ">
### What is straight-line depreciation?
Straight-line depreciation spreads an asset’s cost evenly over its useful life. It’s the simplest method and most commonly used for financial reporting.

### What is double declining balance (DDB)?
DDB is an accelerated method that applies double the straight-line rate to the asset’s declining book value each year. It’s ideal for assets that lose value quickly.

### Can I use this for UK tax purposes?
No. The UK uses **capital allowances** (e.g., Annual Investment Allowance, Writing Down Allowances), not depreciation, for tax. This calculator is for **accounting/book purposes** or **US tax (MACRS)**.

### What is MACRS?
MACRS (Modified Accelerated Cost Recovery System) is the US tax depreciation system. It uses predefined tables with asset classes (3-year, 5-year, etc.) and assumes a half-year convention.

### How do I choose a depreciation method?
- **Financial statements**: Straight-line for simplicity  
- **Tax (US)**: MACRS (required)  
- **Matching usage**: DDB or SYD for high early wear  
- **Conservatism**: Accelerated methods reduce early profits

### Does land depreciate?
No. Land is not depreciable because it does not wear out or become obsolete. Only buildings and improvements on land are depreciated.

### Can I change methods later?
Yes, but only **prospectively** (for future years). You cannot restate prior years’ depreciation.

### What is book value?
Book value = Original cost – Accumulated depreciation. It’s the asset’s value on your balance sheet.
