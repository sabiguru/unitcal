## The Core Principle: Proportional Relationships

A ratio compares two or more quantities by division. A proportion states that two ratios are equal.

**Basic ratio:** 3:4 means "3 parts to 4 parts" or "3 for every 4."

**Equivalent ratios:** 3:4 = 6:8 = 9:12 = 15:20. Multiplying or dividing both terms by the same number creates equivalent ratios.

**Simplifying ratios:** Find the Greatest Common Divisor (GCD) of both terms and divide by it. 18:24 → GCD is 6 → 3:4.

**Proportions:** If a:b = c:d, then a×d = b×c (cross-multiplication). This lets you solve for unknown values.

**Scaling:** To scale a ratio up or down, multiply all terms by the same factor. 2:3 scaled by 4 becomes 8:12.

Ratios are fundamental to mixing, cooking, finance, construction, and countless other applications where maintaining proportions matters.

## How to Work with Ratios Manually

**Simplifying a ratio:**
Find GCD and divide both terms.

Example: Simplify 48:64
Factors of 48: 1, 2, 3, 4, 6, 8, 12, 16, 24, 48
Factors of 64: 1, 2, 4, 8, 16, 32, 64
GCD = 16
48 ÷ 16 = 3, 64 ÷ 16 = 4
Result: 3:4

**Solving a proportion:**
Use cross-multiplication.

Example: 5:7 = x:49
5 × 49 = 7 × x
245 = 7x
x = 35
Verify: 5:7 = 35:49 (both reduce to 5:7) ✓

**Scaling a ratio:**
Multiply all terms by the same factor.

Example: Scale 2:3:5 to make the first term 10
Factor = 10 ÷ 2 = 5
2 × 5 = 10, 3 × 5 = 15, 5 × 5 = 25
Result: 10:15:25

**Converting ratio to fractions:**
3:4 means 3 parts + 4 parts = 7 total parts
First quantity = 3/7 of total
Second quantity = 4/7 of total

Example: Split £70 in ratio 3:4
First share = £70 × 3/7 = £30
Second share = £70 × 4/7 = £40

## Real-World Applications

**Cooking and recipes.** A recipe for 4 servings needs scaling to 10 servings. Multiply all ingredients by 10/4 = 2.5.

**Mixing concrete.** Cement:sand:gravel in 1:2:4 ratio. For 6 buckets of gravel: cement = 6 × (1/4) = 1.5 buckets.

**Financial ratios.** Debt-to-equity ratio, price-to-earnings ratio, and current ratio assess company health.

**Map scales.** A 1:50,000 map means 1 cm represents 50,000 cm (500 m) in reality.

**Architecture and design.** Golden ratio (≈1:1.618) appears in aesthetically pleasing proportions.

**Paint mixing.** Mix colors in specific ratios: 3 parts blue to 1 part yellow creates a specific green.

## Scenarios People Actually Run Into

**The concrete mixing problem.** You have 6 buckets of gravel and need to find cement and sand for 1:2:4 ratio. Gravel is 4 parts, so 1 part = 6/4 = 1.5. Cement = 1.5, Sand = 3, Gravel = 6.

**The recipe scaling trap.** Doubling all ingredients works for most recipes, but some ingredients (like baking powder) don't scale linearly. Know which ratios hold and which don't.

**The aspect ratio distortion.** Resizing an image from 4:3 to 16:9 without proper cropping or letterboxing stretches the image. Maintain aspect ratio to avoid distortion.

**The decimal ratio.** 0.8:2 doesn't look like a clean ratio. Multiply both by 10: 8:20. Simplify by GCD 4: 2:5.

**The multi-part ratio.** Dividing £100 among three people in ratio 2:3:5. Total parts = 10. Shares: £20, £30, £50.

## Trade-Offs and Decisions People Underestimate

**Simplest form versus context.** 1:2 is simpler than 50:100, but sometimes context requires specific numbers (like 50% market share).

**Part-to-part versus part-to-whole.** 3:4 is part-to-part. 3:7 would be part-to-whole (if total is 7). Know which your context requires.

**Exact versus rounded.** Scaling by 7/3 creates fractions. In practice, you might round—but this slightly changes the actual ratio.

**Unit consistency.** Ratios require same units. 2 kg to 500 g isn't 2:500; convert first (2000:500 = 4:1).

**Ratio as fraction.** 3:4 can represent "3 divided by 4" (0.75) or "3 parts to 4 parts." Context determines interpretation.

## Common Mistakes and How to Recover

**Confusing ratio and fraction.** A ratio 3:4 compares parts (3 to 4). A fraction 3/4 compares part to whole. Ratio 3:4 means fractions 3/7 and 4/7 of the whole.

**Not converting units.** 2 meters to 50 cm isn't 2:50. Convert: 200 cm to 50 cm = 200:50 = 4:1.

**Wrong cross-multiplication.** In a:b = c:d, cross-multiply as a×d = b×c, not a×c = b×d.

**Incomplete simplification.** 24:36 → 4:6 is not fully simplified. Continue: 4:6 → 2:3.

**Order reversal.** Ratio 3:4 is different from 4:3. Maintain consistent order based on what you're comparing.

## Related Topics

**Proportions.** Equations stating two ratios are equal. Fundamental to scaling and similarity.

**Direct and inverse proportion.** Direct: as one increases, the other increases (distance:time at constant speed). Inverse: as one increases, the other decreases (speed:time for fixed distance).

**Percentage.** A specific ratio comparing to 100. 25% = 25:100 = 1:4.

**Similar figures.** Geometric shapes with equal ratios of corresponding sides. Same shape, different size.

**Golden ratio.** Approximately 1:1.618, found in nature, art, and architecture as an aesthetically pleasing proportion.

## How This Calculator Works

**Greatest Common Divisor (Euclidean algorithm):**
```
gcd(a, b):
  while b ≠ 0:
    temp = b
    b = a mod b
    a = temp
  return a
```

**Simplify ratio:**
```
divisor = gcd(term1, term2)
simplified1 = term1 / divisor
simplified2 = term2 / divisor
```

**Solve proportion (a:b = c:x):**
```
x = (b × c) / a
```

**Scale ratio:**
```
factor = targetValue / originalValue
newTerm1 = term1 × factor
newTerm2 = term2 × factor
```

**Multi-term ratio scaling:**
```
for each term:
  scaledTerm = term × (knownValue / correspondingPart)
```

## FAQs

**How do I simplify a ratio with decimals?**

Multiply both terms by 10, 100, etc., to eliminate decimals, then find GCD. Example: 0.8:2 → 8:20 → 2:5.

**Can this handle three-part ratios?**

Yes. Enter ratios like 1:2:4 for concrete mix or investment allocation.

**What if I only know one part of a proportion?**

Enter the known ratio and one value from the second ratio. The calculator solves for the missing term using cross-multiplication.

**Is a ratio the same as a fraction?**

Related but different. Ratio 3:4 compares parts (3 to 4). As a fraction, it can represent 3/4 = 0.75, or the parts 3/7 and 4/7 of a whole.

**How do I scale a recipe for different servings?**

Find the scaling factor: new servings / original servings. Multiply all ingredient quantities by this factor.

**Can I use this for aspect ratios?**

Yes. Enter screen dimensions to simplify (1920:1080 → 16:9) or find equivalent dimensions.

**Why does my simplified ratio have decimals?**

If inputs are decimal, output may be too. Multiply to get whole numbers first, then simplify.

**How do I divide an amount in a given ratio?**

Sum the ratio parts, divide total amount by this sum, then multiply by each part. Example: £100 in 2:3 ratio = £100/5 = £20 per part = £40 and £60.

**What's a rate versus a ratio?**

A rate compares quantities with different units (miles per hour, price per pound). A ratio compares quantities with the same unit (3 apples to 4 apples). Both use the colon notation.

**How do I convert a ratio to a percentage?**

For ratio a:b, percentage of first = a/(a+b) × 100. Ratio 3:7 means 3/10 = 30% for first part, 70% for second.

**What is direct proportion?**

When one quantity increases, the other increases at the same rate. If y = kx for constant k, y and x are directly proportional.

**What is inverse proportion?**

When one quantity increases, the other decreases proportionally. If y = k/x for constant k, y and x are inversely proportional.

**How do similar triangles relate to ratios?**

Similar triangles have equal ratios of corresponding sides. This principle enables surveying, shadow calculations, and geometric scaling.

**What is the golden ratio?**

Approximately 1:1.618 (φ). A rectangle with these proportions is aesthetically pleasing. Found in nature, art, architecture, and the Fibonacci sequence.

**How do gear ratios work?**

A 2:1 gear ratio means the driven gear rotates once for every two rotations of the driving gear. This trades speed for torque or vice versa.

**What are financial ratios?**

Metrics comparing financial statement items: debt-to-equity, current ratio, profit margin. Used to assess company health and compare across industries.

**How do I compare ratios?**

Convert to the same form: decimals (3:4 = 0.75, 5:6 = 0.833) or common denominators. Or cross-multiply: a:b vs c:d, compare ad to bc.

**What is unit rate?**

A ratio with denominator 1. "60 miles per hour" is unit rate (60:1). Useful for direct comparison and calculations.

**How do aspect ratios affect displays?**

Screen dimensions in ratio form: 16:9, 4:3, 21:9. Content must match aspect ratio to avoid letterboxing, pillarboxing, or stretching.

**What is a mixing ratio versus a dilution ratio?**

Mixing ratio: parts combined (1:4 means 1 part A + 4 parts B = 5 parts total). Dilution: final volume relative to concentrate (1:4 dilution = 1 part concentrate in 4 parts total).

**How do exchange rates relate to ratios?**

Currency exchange rates are ratios: $1 = €0.92 is ratio 1:0.92 or approximately 100:92. Use cross-multiplication to convert amounts.

**What is odds ratio in probability?**

Odds compare success to failure: 3:1 odds means 3 successes for every 1 failure, or 75% probability. Different from probability (3/4).

**How do ratios apply to music and harmony?**

Musical intervals are frequency ratios. Octave = 2:1, perfect fifth = 3:2. These simple ratios create harmonious sounds.

**What is dimensional analysis using ratios?**

Convert units by multiplying by ratios equal to 1. To convert 60 mph to ft/s: 60 miles/hour × 5280 feet/mile × 1 hour/3600 seconds = 88 ft/s.

**Additional Notes**

Ratios underlie countless practical applications from cooking to engineering. Understanding how to manipulate and apply ratios enables accurate scaling, fair distribution, and proper proportions in any quantitative task.
