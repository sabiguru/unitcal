<TabPanel title="Quick Guide">
Our **dog age calculator** converts your dog’s age to human years using the modern, breed-size-based method endorsed by veterinarians. Forget the outdated “multiply by 7” rule—this tool gives you an accurate equivalent that reflects your dog’s true developmental stage, helping you provide better care and understand their needs.

### How It Works
The calculator uses a four-category model based on adult weight:
- **Small** (under 20 lbs)
- **Medium** (21–50 lbs)
- **Large** (51–90 lbs)
- **Giant** (>90 lbs)

Key milestones:
- **1 dog year** = **15 human years**
- **2 dog years** = **24 human years**
- **Each year after**: Adds 4–8 human years depending on size

This reflects that small dogs age slower after maturity than large breeds.

### Why It Matters
Knowing your dog’s human-equivalent age helps you:
- Choose the right diet (puppy, adult, senior)
- Anticipate health screenings (e.g., kidney tests for seniors)
- Understand behaviour changes (e.g., reduced play in older dogs)
- Plan vet visit frequency (annual for adults, biannual for seniors)

### How to Use
1. Enter your dog’s **age in years** (use 0.5 for 6 months, etc.).
2. Select their **adult weight category** (use ideal weight, not current if overweight).
3. The calculator instantly shows:
   - Human-equivalent age
   - Life stage (Puppy, Adult, Senior, Geriatric)
   - Key care recommendations for that stage

This method aligns with guidelines from the American Kennel Club and veterinary gerontology research.

<TabPanel title="FAQ">
### Is the “dog years × 7” rule accurate?
No. It’s a myth. Dogs age rapidly in their first two years (15 human years at age 1, 24 at age 2), then slow down. Crucially, **size matters**—small dogs age slower than large ones after age 2.

### How do I know my dog’s weight category?
Use their **ideal adult weight**:
- **Small**: under 20 lbs (e.g., Chihuahua, Dachshund)
- **Medium**: 21–50 lbs (e.g., Beagle, Border Collie)
- **Large**: 51–90 lbs (e.g., Labrador, German Shepherd)
- **Giant**: >90 lbs (e.g., Great Dane, Mastiff)

If unsure, consult your vet.

### When is a dog considered a senior?
It depends on size:
- **Giant breeds**: 5–6 years
- **Large breeds**: 6–7 years
- **Medium breeds**: 7–8 years
- **Small breeds**: 10–12 years

### Why do small dogs live longer?
Larger dogs grow faster, which may accelerate cellular aging and increase cancer risk. Their bodies also work harder to maintain size, leading to earlier age-related decline.

### Should I change my dog’s food at a certain age?
Yes. Transition to **adult food at 1 year**, and consider **senior food** based on size and age (7–10 years). Senior formulas support joints, kidneys, and digestion.

### How often should senior dogs see the vet?
**Every 6 months**. Dogs hide illness well, and early detection of conditions like arthritis or kidney disease significantly improves quality of life.

### Can I use this for puppies?
Yes. For puppies under 1 year, the calculator uses monthly milestones (e.g., 6 months ≈ 10 human years) to reflect rapid development.

### Does breed affect aging?
Yes. Some breeds have longer average lifespans (e.g., Jack Russell: 13–16 years), but **size is the strongest predictor** of aging rate. Always use weight category for conversion.
