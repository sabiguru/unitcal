# How to Convert Units — Metric & Imperial Guide

## Introduction
Unit conversion is a fundamental skill that bridges the gap between measurement systems used across the globe, in science, engineering, cooking, and everyday life. Whether you’re following a British recipe that uses grams while your kitchen scale shows ounces, interpreting a US car manual that lists torque in pound-feet, or calculating the dose of a medication prescribed in milligrams, knowing **how to convert units** accurately is essential. A **unit converter** provides instant results, but understanding the manual method builds confidence, prevents errors, and ensures you can adapt when digital tools aren’t available.

This comprehensive guide explains the principles behind unit conversion, from the logical structure of the **metric system** to the historical quirks of **imperial units**. You’ll learn how to use **conversion factors**, navigate **unit prefixes**, and handle complex conversions involving area, volume, and compound units (like speed or density). With step-by-step examples and practical tips, you’ll gain the ability to move seamlessly between systems—turning a potential source of confusion into a tool for global literacy.

## The Two Worlds of Measurement: Metric vs. Imperial
### The Metric System: Decimal Simplicity
The **metric system** (SI) is based on powers of 10, making conversions straightforward:
- **Base units**: metre (length), kilogram (mass), litre (volume), second (time)
- **Prefixes** scale units up or down:
  - **kilo- (k)** = 1,000 (e.g., 1 km = 1,000 m)
  - **centi- (c)** = 0.01 (e.g., 1 cm = 0.01 m)
  - **milli- (m)** = 0.001 (e.g., 1 mm = 0.001 m)

To convert, simply move the decimal point:
- **km → m**: multiply by 1,000 (3.2 km = 3,200 m)
- **g → kg**: divide by 1,000 (450 g = 0.45 kg)

### The Imperial System: Historical Complexity
The **imperial system** (used primarily in the US and UK for certain applications) relies on less intuitive ratios:
- **Length**: 12 inches = 1 foot; 3 feet = 1 yard; 1,760 yards = 1 mile
- **Mass**: 16 ounces = 1 pound; 14 pounds = 1 stone (UK); 2,000 pounds = 1 short ton (US)
- **Volume**: 8 fluid ounces = 1 cup; 2 cups = 1 pint; 2 pints = 1 quart; 4 quarts = 1 gallon

Conversions require memorisation or reference to conversion factors.

## The Universal Method: Dimensional Analysis
The most reliable way to convert any unit is **dimensional analysis** (also called the factor-label method). It uses **conversion factors**—fractions equal to 1—to cancel unwanted units.

### Step-by-Step Process
1. **Write the given value with its unit**.
2. **Multiply by a conversion factor** that cancels the original unit and introduces the desired unit.
3. **Cancel units** and compute the numerical result.

**Example**: Convert 5 miles to kilometres.  
- Conversion factor: 1 mile = 1.60934 km → `1.60934 km / 1 mile`  
- Calculation: `5 miles × (1.60934 km / 1 mile) = 8.0467 km`  
- **Answer**: **8.05 km** (rounded)

### Handling Compound Units
For units like **speed** (m/s, mph) or **density** (kg/m³), convert each part separately.

**Example**: Convert 60 mph to m/s.  
- 1 mile = 1,609.34 m  
- 1 hour = 3,600 seconds  
- Calculation:  
  `60 miles/hour × (1,609.34 m / 1 mile) × (1 hour / 3,600 s) = 26.82 m/s`

## Common Conversion Categories & Key Factors
### Length
| From → To       | Conversion Factor        |
|------------------|--------------------------|
| Inches → cm      | 1 in = 2.54 cm           |
| Feet → m         | 1 ft = 0.3048 m          |
| Miles → km       | 1 mi = 1.60934 km        |
| Yards → m        | 1 yd = 0.9144 m          |

### Mass
| From → To       | Conversion Factor        |
|------------------|--------------------------|
| Pounds → kg      | 1 lb = 0.453592 kg       |
| Ounces → g       | 1 oz = 28.3495 g         |
| Stones → kg      | 1 st = 6.35029 kg        |

### Volume
| From → To               | Conversion Factor        |
|--------------------------|--------------------------|
| US Gallons → Litres      | 1 gal = 3.78541 L        |
| UK Gallons → Litres      | 1 gal = 4.54609 L        |
| Fluid Ounces → mL        | 1 fl oz = 29.5735 mL     |
| Cubic Feet → m³          | 1 ft³ = 0.0283168 m³     |

### Temperature
- **Celsius to Fahrenheit**: `°F = (°C × 9/5) + 32`  
- **Fahrenheit to Celsius**: `°C = (°F − 32) × 5/9`  
- **Celsius to Kelvin**: `K = °C + 273.15`

### Area and Volume
- **Area**: Square the linear conversion factor  
  (e.g., 1 m² = (100 cm)² = 10,000 cm²)
- **Volume**: Cube the linear conversion factor  
  (e.g., 1 m³ = (100 cm)³ = 1,000,000 cm³)

## Pro Tips for Accurate Conversions
- **Use exact conversion factors** when precision matters (e.g., 1 inch = 2.54 cm exactly).
- **Round only the final answer**—carry extra digits through intermediate steps.
- **Check unit cancellation**: If your final unit isn’t what you expected, your conversion factor is inverted.
- **Beware of US vs. UK gallons**: A UK gallon is ~20% larger than a US gallon.
- **For cooking**: Use weight (grams) instead of volume (cups) for dry ingredients to avoid density errors.

## Worked Examples & Practice Scenarios

### Example 1: Cooking Conversion (Imperial → Metric)
**Problem**: A US recipe calls for **2 cups of flour**. How many grams is that?  
(Assume 1 cup all-purpose flour ≈ 120 g)

**Solution**:  
`2 cups × 120 g/cup = 240 g`  
✅ **Answer**: **240 grams**

### Example 2: Travel Distance (Miles → Kilometres)
**Problem**: A road sign in the US says “Next town: 150 miles”. How far is that in km?

**Solution**:  
`150 mi × 1.60934 km/mi = 241.4 km`  
✅ **Answer**: **241 km**

### Example 3: Temperature Conversion
**Problem**: The weather forecast in London is **18°C**. What’s that in Fahrenheit?

**Solution**:  
`°F = (18 × 9/5) + 32 = 32.4 + 32 = 64.4°F`  
✅ **Answer**: **64°F**

### Example 4: Compound Unit (Speed)
**Problem**: A car’s speedometer reads **100 km/h**. What is that in mph?

**Solution**:  
`100 km/h × (1 mi / 1.60934 km) = 62.14 mph`  
✅ **Answer**: **62 mph**

### Example 5: Volume (Gallons → Litres)
**Problem**: A UK fuel tank holds **50 litres**. How many UK gallons is that?

**Solution**:  
`50 L ÷ 4.54609 L/gal = 11.0 UK gallons`  
✅ **Answer**: **11 gallons**

### Practice Problems (Try These!)
1. Convert **5 feet 10 inches** to centimetres.  
2. Convert **80 kg** to pounds.  
3. Convert **25°C** to Kelvin.  
4. Convert **10 m/s** to km/h.

**Answers**:  
1. (5×12 + 10) × 2.54 = 70 × 2.54 = **178 cm**  
2. 80 ÷ 0.453592 ≈ **176 lb**  
3. 25 + 273.15 = **298.15 K**  
4. 10 × 3.6 = **36 km/h**

### What’s the difference between US and UK gallons?
- **US gallon** = 3.785 litres  
- **UK (imperial) gallon** = 4.546 litres  
This is critical for fuel economy (MPG) comparisons—UK MPG is ~20% higher than US MPG for the same car.

### How do I convert between Celsius and Fahrenheit quickly?
Use these approximations for mental math:
- **°C to °F**: Double °C and add 30 (e.g., 20°C ≈ 70°F; actual = 68°F)
- **°F to °C**: Subtract 30 and halve (e.g., 70°F ≈ 20°C; actual = 21.1°C)  
Good for weather, not for science.

### Why is 1 inch exactly 2.54 cm?
In 1959, the US and UK agreed to define the inch as **exactly 2.54 cm** to standardise international measurements. This is now a fixed conversion.

### Can I convert area or volume by squaring or cubing linear factors?
Yes!  
- **Area**: (1 m = 100 cm) → 1 m² = 10,000 cm²  
- **Volume**: 1 m³ = 1,000,000 cm³  
This avoids common errors like thinking 1 m² = 100 cm².

### What are the most common unit conversion mistakes?
1. **Confusing mass and weight** (kg vs. lb-force)
2. **Mixing US and UK gallons or fluid ounces**
3. **Forgetting to square/cube for area/volume**
4. **Inverting conversion factors** (e.g., multiplying instead of dividing)
5. **Using approximate factors in precision contexts**

### How do I convert pressure units (psi, bar, kPa)?
- 1 psi = 6.89476 kPa  
- 1 bar = 100 kPa = 14.5038 psi  
Car tyre pressures are often listed in both psi and bar.

### Is there a universal conversion method?
Yes—**dimensional analysis** (factor-label method) works for any unit, including complex ones like energy (joules vs. calories) or power (watts vs. horsepower).

### Why do scientists use SI units?
The **International System of Units (SI)** is coherent, decimal-based, and universally accepted in science. It eliminates ambiguity and simplifies calculations across disciplines and countries.
