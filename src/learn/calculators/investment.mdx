## The Core Investment Growth Formula
The foundation of investment projection combines a starting balance with regular contributions, all growing at a compound rate:

**Future Value (FV) = P(1 + r/n)^(nt) + PMT × [((1 + r/n)^(nt) – 1) / (r/n)]**

Where:
- **`P`** = Initial investment (principal)
- **`PMT`** = Regular contribution (e.g., monthly deposit)
- **`r`** = Annual rate of return (as a decimal)
- **`n`** = Compounding periods per year (e.g., 12 for monthly)
- **`t`** = Time in years

This formula has two parts:
1. **Lump sum growth**: `P(1 + r/n)^(nt)`
2. **Annuity growth**: `PMT × [((1 + r/n)^(nt) – 1) / (r/n)]`

### Accounting for Inflation: Real vs. Nominal Returns
Nominal returns show raw growth; real returns reflect **purchasing power** after inflation.

**Real Return ≈ Nominal Return – Inflation Rate**  
For precision:  
**Real Value = Nominal FV / (1 + Inflation Rate)^t**

*Example*:  
- Nominal FV: £500,000  
- Inflation: 2.5% over 30 years  
- Real Value: `500,000 / (1.025)^30 ≈ £237,000`  
Your portfolio doubled nominally but lost 53% of its purchasing power.

### Contribution Timing: Start vs. End of Period
- **End-of-period** (ordinary annuity): Standard for most calculators
- **Start-of-period** (annuity due): Each contribution compounds one period longer  
  → Multiply annuity term by `(1 + r/n)`

### Tax Considerations
- **Taxable accounts**: Returns are reduced by capital gains/dividend taxes
- **Tax-advantaged accounts** (ISAs, SIPPs): Growth is tax-free or tax-deferred  
Always model **net returns** (after fees and taxes) for accuracy.

## Step-by-Step Investment Planning Process
1. **Define your goal**: Retirement (£1M), house deposit (£50K), etc.
2. **Estimate time horizon**: Years until you need the money
3. **Choose a realistic return**: 
   - **Conservative**: 4–5% (bonds, cash)
   - **Balanced**: 6–7% (60/40 stock/bond mix)
   - **Aggressive**: 8–9% (100% equities)
4. **Factor in fees**: Subtract 0.25–1% for fund expenses
5. **Solve for missing variable**: 
   - If you know your goal, solve for **monthly contribution**
   - If you know your contribution, solve for **future value**

## Pro Tips & Best Practices
- **Use conservative return assumptions**: Overestimating returns leads to under-saving
- **Automate contributions**: Ensures consistency and leverages dollar-cost averaging
- **Rebalance annually**: Maintains target asset allocation as markets fluctuate
- **Increase contributions with raises**: Allocate 50% of salary increases to investments
- **Prioritize tax-advantaged accounts**: Max out ISAs/SIPPs before taxable accounts
- **Review annually**: Adjust for life changes (marriage, children, job loss)

## Practice Calculating Investment Scenarios

### Scenario 1: The Power of Starting Early
- **Early Investor**: Starts at 25 with £5,000, adds £300/month at 7% for 40 years  
  → FV = **£720,000**  
- **Late Investor**: Starts at 35 with £5,000, adds £300/month at 7% for 30 years  
  → FV = **£340,000**  
**Insight**: Starting 10 years earlier yields **£380,000 more**—despite identical contributions.

### Scenario 2: Solving for Monthly Contributions
**Goal**: £1,000,000 in 35 years  
- Current savings: £20,000  
- Expected return: 7%  
- **Required monthly contribution**: **£480**  
*Formula*: Solve `1,000,000 = 20,000(1.07)^35 + PMT × [((1.07)^35 – 1)/0.07]`

### Scenario 3: Inflation Impact
- **Nominal goal**: £800,000 in 25 years  
- **Inflation**: 3%  
- **Real purchasing power**: `800,000 / (1.03)^25 = **£382,000**`  
To maintain today’s £800K purchasing power, you’d need **£1.67M** nominally.

### Scenario 4: Fee Drag
- **Gross return**: 8%  
- **Fees**: 1%  
- **Net return**: 7%  
Over 30 years, 1% in fees reduces final balance by **25–30%**.

### Scenario 5: Catch-Up Investing
After 10 years of no savings, you have 25 years left to reach £500,000.  
- **Required monthly contribution at 7%**: **£620**  
(vs. £350 if started on time)

### What’s the difference between this and a compound interest calculator?
A **compound interest calculator** models a single lump sum. An **investment calculator** adds **regular contributions**, making it essential for real-world goals like retirement or education savings.

### What’s a realistic rate of return?
- **Conservative**: 4–5% (for bonds/cash-heavy portfolios)  
- **Balanced**: 6–7% (for 60/40 stock/bond mixes)  
- **Aggressive**: 8–9% (for 100% equities)  
Always use **net returns** (after 0.25–1% fees). Historical S&P 500 average is 10% gross, but includes high volatility.

### How do I account for taxes?
- **ISAs/SIPPs**: Use gross returns (tax-free growth)  
- **Taxable accounts**: Reduce returns by estimated tax rate (e.g., 10–20% for dividends/capital gains)  
For precision, consult a tax advisor—rules vary by country and account type.

### Should I assume end-of-month or start-of-month contributions?
**Start-of-month** is slightly better (extra compounding), but the difference is minor (below 1% over 30 years). Consistency matters more than timing. Most payroll deductions (e.g., 401(k)) function as start-of-period contributions.

### How does inflation affect my investments?
Inflation erodes purchasing power. A 3% inflation rate halves your money’s value in 24 years. Always:  
1. Use **real returns** (nominal – inflation) for goal planning  
2. Invest in assets that outpace inflation (stocks, real estate)

### Can I model irregular contributions?
This calculator assumes fixed contributions. For irregular deposits (bonuses, windfalls):  
1. Calculate base scenario with regular contributions  
2. Add lump sums manually using compound interest formula  
3. Sum the results

### What if my returns are volatile?
The calculator assumes smooth, constant returns. Real markets fluctuate—some years +20%, others -10%. The projected result is a **long-term average**. To model volatility:  
- Use Monte Carlo simulations (advanced tools)  
- Apply a 10–20% safety margin to your target

### How often should I revisit my plan?
- **Annually**: Review progress and adjust contributions  
- **After major life events**: Marriage, children, job changes  
- **When markets shift**: Rebalance if asset allocation drifts >5% from target
