## The Core Salary Calculations: From Hourly to Annual
All salary conversions start with a few key inputs and standardised assumptions about working hours.

### 1. Hourly to Annual Salary
This is the most common conversion, used to compare part-time or contract roles with full-time positions.

**Formula**:  
`Annual Salary = Hourly Wage × Hours per Week × Weeks per Year`

**Standard Assumptions**:
- **Full-time**: 37.5–40 hours/week (UK average is 36.6 hrs, but 37.5 is common for salaried roles)
- **Weeks per year**: 52 (includes paid holiday)

*Example*: £18/hour, 37.5 hrs/week:  
`18 × 37.5 × 52 = **£35,100/year**`

> 💡 **Pro Tip**: If you take unpaid leave, reduce weeks accordingly (e.g., 50 weeks for 2 weeks unpaid).

### 2. Annual Salary to Other Pay Periods
Once you have the annual figure, breaking it down is straightforward—but **pay frequency matters**.

| Pay Frequency | Calculation | Example (£35,100/year) |
|--------------|------------|------------------------|
| **Monthly** | Annual ÷ 12 | £2,925 |
| **Bi-weekly** | Annual ÷ 26 | £1,350 |
| **Weekly** | Annual ÷ 52 | £675 |

> ⚠️ **Key Distinction**:  
> - **Bi-weekly** = every 2 weeks (26 pays/year)  
> - **Semi-monthly** = twice a month (24 pays/year → £1,462.50)

### 3. Overtime Calculations
In the UK, overtime is typically paid at **1.5×** the hourly rate for hours beyond the contracted week.

**Formula**:  
`Overtime Pay = Overtime Hours × Hourly Rate × 1.5`  
`Total Weekly Pay = (Regular Hours × Rate) + Overtime Pay`

*Example*: Contracted 37.5 hrs at £20/hr, work 45 hrs:  
- Regular: `37.5 × 20 = £750`  
- Overtime: `(45 – 37.5) × 20 × 1.5 = 7.5 × 30 = £225`  
- **Total**: **£975/week**

### 4. Total Compensation: Beyond Base Pay
Your true earnings include:
- **Bonuses** (annual, performance-based)
- **Commission** (sales roles)
- **Benefits** (pension contributions, health insurance)
- **Paid Time Off** (holiday, sick leave)

> 📌 **Rule of Thumb**: A 5% employer pension contribution adds **£1,755** to a £35,100 salary.

## Pro Tips & Common Mistakes
- **Clarify pay frequency**: A £2,500 semi-monthly paycheck ≠ £2,500 bi-weekly.
- **Budget on net pay**: Use a [Tax Calculator](/calculator/financial/tax) to estimate take-home pay after income tax, National Insurance, and pension deductions.
- **Calculate effective hourly rate**:  
  `Annual Salary ÷ (Hours/Week × Weeks/Year)`  
  Helps compare salaried vs. hourly roles.
- **Model scenarios**: See how a £2,000 raise or 5 hrs/week overtime impacts your annual income.
- **Consider non-monetary value**: Remote work, flexible hours, and job security are part of total compensation.

### How do I convert annual salary to hourly rate in the UK?
Divide your annual salary by your total working hours per year. For a standard 37.5-hour week:  
`Hourly Rate = Annual Salary ÷ (37.5 × 52) = Annual Salary ÷ 1,950`  
*Example*: £39,000 ÷ 1,950 = **£20/hour**

### What’s the difference between bi-weekly and semi-monthly pay?
- **Bi-weekly**: Paid every 2 weeks → **26 pays/year**  
- **Semi-monthly**: Paid twice a month (e.g., 1st and 15th) → **24 pays/year**  
A semi-monthly paycheck is larger, but you receive 2 fewer per year.

### How is overtime calculated in the UK?
While UK law doesn’t mandate overtime pay, most employers pay **time-and-a-half (1.5×)** for hours beyond the contracted week. Always check your employment contract.

### Should I include bonuses in my salary calculation?
For **job comparisons**, yes—include typical bonuses to assess total compensation. For **budgeting**, be conservative—exclude bonuses or use a 3-year average.

### How do taxes affect my take-home pay?
UK income tax and National Insurance reduce your gross pay. On a £35,000 salary:
- **Income Tax**: £3,480 (after £12,570 personal allowance)
- **NI**: £2,452 (12% on £35,000 – £12,570)  
- **Net Pay**: ~£29,068/year (**£2,422/month**)

### What is a good salary in the UK?
It depends on location and role. As of 2025:
- **National average**: ~£34,000  
- **London average**: ~£45,000  
- **Living wage**: £12.00/hr (£24,960 full-time)

### Can I calculate my salary with irregular hours?
Yes—track your hours over 3 months, calculate average weekly hours, then apply the hourly-to-annual formula.

### How do I factor in pension contributions?
- **Employee contributions** reduce your net pay  
- **Employer contributions** are part of your total compensation  
Always include both when comparing job offers.
