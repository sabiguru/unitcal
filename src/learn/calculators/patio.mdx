## The Four Pillars of Patio Material Calculation
Every successful patio rests on four calculated components:
1. **Pavers** – The visible surface
2. **Gravel Base** – The structural foundation
3. **Bedding Sand** – The leveling layer
4. **Edging & Jointing** – The finishing touches

### 1. Calculating Patio Area: The Starting Point
All calculations begin with **total area** in square feet (or square metres).

- **Rectangle/Square**: `Area = Length × Width`
- **Circle**: `Area = π × Radius²` (Radius = Diameter ÷ 2)
- **Irregular Shapes**: Break into rectangles, triangles, or circles, then sum.

> 📏 **Pro Tip**: Measure twice. Add 6–12 inches to each dimension if your patio abuts a house or fence—this accounts for excavation overhang.

### 2. How Many Pavers Do You Need?
This depends on **paver size** and **pattern complexity**.

#### Step-by-Step:
1. **Paver Area** = (Length in inches × Width in inches) ÷ 144  
   *(Converts sq in → sq ft)*  
   *Example: 6" × 9" = 54 ÷ 144 = 0.375 sq ft per paver*
2. **Basic Count** = Total Patio Area ÷ Paver Area
3. **Add Waste Factor**:
   - **7–10%** for simple patterns (running bond, grid)
   - **10–15%** for complex patterns (herringbone, basketweave) or curved edges
4. **Total Pavers** = Basic Count × (1 + Waste Factor)

> 💡 **Why waste?** Cuts for edges, breakage during installation, and future repairs.

### 3. Base Materials: Gravel and Sand
A stable base prevents sinking, cracking, and weed growth.

#### Gravel Base (Crushed Stone):
- **Depth**: 4–6 inches (compacted)
  - 4" for pedestrian use on stable soil
  - 6" for clay soil, freeze-thaw zones, or heavy furniture
- **Volume (cu ft)** = Patio Area × (Depth in inches ÷ 12)
- **Convert to Cubic Yards**: ÷ 27 (since 1 yd³ = 27 ft³)

#### Bedding Sand:
- **Depth**: 1 inch of coarse, sharp sand (not play sand)
- **Volume (cu ft)** = Patio Area × (1 ÷ 12) = Patio Area ÷ 12

### 4. Edging, Drainage, and Finishing
- **Edge Restraint**: Measure the **perimeter** in linear feet. Essential to lock pavers in place.
- **Drainage Slope**: Patio must slope **away from your house** at **1/4 inch per foot** (2% grade).  
  *Example: 12-ft deep patio → 3-inch drop at far end.*
- **Polymeric Sand**: Fills joints to prevent weeds and lock pavers.  
  - Coverage: 50–100 sq ft per 50lb bag (depends on joint width)

## Pro Tips & Common Mistakes
- **Don’t skip geotextile fabric**: Lay landscape fabric under gravel on clay or weedy soil.
- **Compact in layers**: Add gravel in 2-inch lifts; compact each before adding more.
- **Dry-lay first**: Test your pattern and colour with a small section before full install.
- **Order extra pavers**: Store unused ones for future repairs—colour batches vary.
- **Check local codes**: Some areas require specific base depths or drainage plans.

## Practical Applications
- Compare paver sizes for cost efficiency
- Plan material delivery (gravel/sand sold by cubic yard)
- Budget for tools (plate compactor, masonry saw)
- Design for accessibility (smooth transitions, non-slip surfaces)

## Worked Examples & Practice Problems

### 1. Rectangular Patio (12 ft × 16 ft)
- **Pavers**: 6" × 9"  
- **Waste**: 10%  
- **Base**: 4" gravel, 1" sand

**Calculations**:
- Area = 12 × 16 = **192 sq ft**
- Paver area = (6×9)/144 = **0.375 sq ft**
- Basic pavers = 192 ÷ 0.375 = **512**
- Total pavers = 512 × 1.10 = **563.2 → 564 pavers**
- Gravel = 192 × (4/12) = **64 cu ft** = 64 ÷ 27 ≈ **2.37 yd³ → order 2.5 yd³**
- Sand = 192 ÷ 12 = **16 cu ft** = 16 ÷ 27 ≈ **0.59 yd³ → order 0.75 yd³**
- Edging = 2×(12+16) = **56 linear ft**

### 2. Circular Patio (14 ft diameter)
- Same pavers, 15% waste

**Calculations**:
- Radius = 7 ft
- Area = π × 7² ≈ **153.9 sq ft**
- Basic pavers = 153.9 ÷ 0.375 ≈ **410**
- Total pavers = 410 × 1.15 ≈ **472 pavers**

> Why higher waste? Curved edges require more cuts.

### 3. Cost Comparison: 6×9 vs. 12×12 Pavers
- **6×9**: £0.75 each → 564 × £0.75 = **£423**
- **12×12**: £2.25 each → (192 ÷ 1) × 1.08 × £2.25 ≈ 207 × £2.25 = **£466**

→ Smaller pavers can be more economical despite higher count.

### How do I calculate pavers for an irregular shape?
Break the shape into simple rectangles, triangles, or circles. Calculate each area separately, sum them, then proceed with paver count as usual.

### What type of gravel should I use?
Use **3/4" crushed stone** (also called "road base" or "minus gravel"). It compacts well and drains effectively. Avoid pea gravel—it doesn’t lock together.

### Can I use play sand for the bedding layer?
No. **Play sand is too fine** and holds water. Use **coarse, sharp sand** (often called "paver sand" or "concrete sand")—it drains and locks pavers in place.

### How much extra should I order for a herringbone pattern?
Herringbone requires many angled cuts. Use a **12–15% waste factor**. For large projects, order a full extra pallet if possible.

### Do I need to slope a small patio?
Yes—even a 6 ft × 6 ft patio should slope **away from the house** at 1/4" per foot to prevent water pooling.

### How do I measure slope during installation?
Use a **2-metre straight board and a level with a 13 mm (½ inch) block** at one end. The gap under the board at 2m = 13 mm = correct 2% slope.

### Can I reuse old pavers from a demolished patio?
Yes—if they’re intact. Clean them thoroughly and include them in your count. But still order 5–7% new pavers for cuts and replacements.

### Is a gravel base necessary for a small patio?
Absolutely. Even a 3 ft × 3 ft stepping stone area needs 4" of compacted gravel to prevent sinking and frost heave.
