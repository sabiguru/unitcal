# 1. Pattern Recognition — Spotting Repetition You Don’t Notice

Your habits often repeat quietly:

- You walk more on weekends  
- You snack more after 9pm  
- Your heart rate jumps during certain meetings  
- You sleep less on days you work late  

AI models turn these behaviours into numerical patterns. If your step counts form a vector (e.g., `[6300, 7500, 6900...]`), AI analyses the rhythm. If your calories vary daily, the model detects high-calorie “clusters”.

This is how fitness apps can say things like:  
“You tend to consume more calories on Fridays.”

# 2. Sequence Analysis — Understanding Cause & Effect Over Time  
AI doesn’t just look at single days. It examines sequences:  
**X happens → then Y happens.**

Examples:

- Sleeping past midnight → lower step count the next day  
- High stress day → higher resting heart rate  
- Big workout → lower appetite  
- Long screen time → delayed sleep  
- Eating late → poor recovery  

Sequence models like RNNs and transformers (the same tech powering large language models) excel at reading this type of pattern.

And because many health changes involve *chains* of events, this matters:

- Calories up → weight up  
- Workouts consistent → fitness score up  
- Sleep improves → energy improves

[BMI calculator](https://calcfort.com/calculator/health/bmi)

# 3. Anomaly Detection — Finding What’s “Unusual” For You

One of the most useful things AI does is detect anomalies — not just compared to other people, but compared to *your* baseline.

Examples:

- Resting heart rate suddenly jumps  
- Sleep quality drops sharply  
- Calorie intake spikes  
- Step count falls far below normal  
- Heart rate during workouts rises unusually fast  

This is often how health apps warn you early:

- “You may be coming down with something.”

# When AI Interprets Your Habits Incorrectly  
AI is powerful — but it’s not perfect.

Common failures include:

### **1. Misreading calorie burn**  
Wearables often overestimate.  
Cross-check with Calcfort’s  
[calorie calculator](https://calcfort.com/calculator/health/calorie).

### **2. Oversimplifying weight prediction**  
Weight fluctuates from hydration, hormones, sodium, and stress.  
Check using the  
[BMI calculator](https://calcfort.com/calculator/health/bmi).

### **3. Assuming patterns equal causes**  
Sleeping badly after pizza doesn’t mean pizza ruins your sleep — maybe it was stress.

### **4. Overreacting to anomalies**  
One bad night of sleep doesn’t mean burnout is coming.

### **5. Underreacting to real trends**  
Slowly rising resting heart rate may be ignored by some apps — but it’s meaningful.

This is why calculators matter:  
they give you numbers that AI predictions can’t distort.
