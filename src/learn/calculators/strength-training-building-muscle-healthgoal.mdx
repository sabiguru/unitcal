  ## How to Approach Strength Training

  ### A. The Principles  
  - **Frequency:** 2 – 3 sessions per week hitting all major muscles.  
  - **Progressive overload:** Gradually increase resistance or reps to continue gaining.  
  - **Form before load:** Quality technique prevents injury.  
  - **Rest & recovery:** Growth happens during rest; get 7 – 9 hours of sleep nightly.  

  ### B. Sample 3-Day Plan

  **Day 1 — Upper Body**  
  - Push-ups / Bench press — 3 × 8 – 12  
  - Dumbbell row — 3 × 10  
  - Overhead press — 2 × 10  
  - Lat-pull or pull-ups — 2 × 8  
  - Plank hold — 3 × 30 s  

  **Day 2 — Lower Body**  
  - Squat — 3 × 8  
  - Deadlift — 2 × 6  
  - Lunges — 2 × 10 each leg  
  - Calf raises — 3 × 12  
  - Glute bridge — 2 × 12  

  **Day 3 — Functional / Full Body**  
  - Goblet squat — 3 × 10  
  - Dumbbell swing — 3 × 15  
  - Push-up + row combo — 3 × 8  
  - Farmer’s carry — 2 × 30 m  
  - Core circuit — 2 rounds  

  ### C. Monitoring Progress
  - Re-test lifts every 4–6 weeks.  
  - Check composition with the **[Body Fat Calculator](/calculator/health/bodyfat)**.  
  - Log training volume, recovery, and soreness.

## Science Snapshot

- Strength training boosts insulin sensitivity and lowers type 2 diabetes risk (Health Harvard).  
- It increases bone-density markers, reducing osteoporosis risk in adults over 50.  
- Meta-analyses confirm significant gains in mobility and reduced fall risk in older adults performing resistance work.  
- Even beginners gain measurable muscle within 6 – 8 weeks of consistent training.

## Common Myths

- **“Lifting makes you bulky.”** False — muscle growth is gradual and depends on diet and hormones.  
- **“Cardio is enough.”** Cardio supports heart health, but doesn’t build or preserve muscle.  
- **“I’m too old.”** Strength training benefits every age group — studies show seniors gain strength and balance within 8 weeks.  
- **“I don’t have time.”** Two 30-minute sessions weekly already improve longevity metrics.

## FAQs

**Q: How many days per week are ideal?**  
A: Two to three total-body sessions are enough for most adults.

**Q: Will muscle slow weight loss?**  
A: The scale may stay similar because muscle is denser than fat — check your **[Body Fat %](/calculator/health/bodyfat)** instead.

**Q: Do I need supplements?**  
A: A balanced diet meets most needs; consider whey protein or vitamin D if diet lacks them.

**Q: How fast will I see results?**  
A: Noticeable strength gains appear in 4 – 6 weeks; visible body changes often by 8 – 12 weeks.
