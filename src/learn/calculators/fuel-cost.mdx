## The Science of Fuel Cost Calculation
Fuel cost estimation is a straightforward application of unit conversion and proportional reasoning. The key is understanding your vehicle’s **fuel economy**—how efficiently it converts fuel into distance—and combining it with current fuel prices.

### Core Formulas
**Metric System (Litres per 100 km)**:  
`Fuel Needed (L) = (Distance (km) × Consumption (L/100 km)) ÷ 100`  
`Total Cost = Fuel Needed × Price per Litre`

**Imperial System (Miles per Gallon)**:  
`Fuel Needed (gal) = Distance (mi) ÷ MPG`  
`Total Cost = Fuel Needed × Price per Gallon`

**Cost per Unit Distance**:  
`Cost per km = (Consumption (L/100 km) × Price per L) ÷ 100`  
`Cost per mile = Price per gal ÷ MPG`

*Example (Imperial)*:  
- Trip: 300 miles  
- Car: 25 MPG  
- Fuel: £1.80/gallon  
- Fuel needed: `300 ÷ 25 = 12 gal`  
- Total cost: `12 × £1.80 = **£21.60**`

### Understanding Fuel Economy Metrics
- **MPG (Miles per Gallon)**: Common in the US and UK. **Higher is better**.  
- **L/100 km (Litres per 100 km)**: Standard in Europe and Canada. **Lower is better**.  
- **Conversion**: `L/100 km = 235.215 ÷ MPG` (for US gallons)  
  *Example*: 30 MPG ≈ **7.84 L/100 km**

### Real-World Factors That Affect Accuracy
Theoretical fuel economy rarely matches real-world performance due to:
- **Driving style**: Aggressive acceleration/braking reduces efficiency by 15–30%.
- **Speed**: Fuel economy drops sharply above 55–60 mph (88–96 km/h) due to aerodynamic drag.
- **Load and accessories**: Roof racks, heavy cargo, and AC use increase consumption.
- **Terrain**: Hills and mountains significantly impact fuel use.
- **Vehicle condition**: Under-inflated tires, poor maintenance, and old engines reduce efficiency.
- **Fuel quality**: Lower-grade fuels may reduce engine efficiency.

For accurate estimates, use your **actual observed fuel economy** from recent trips, not the manufacturer’s advertised figure.

## Step-by-Step Fuel Cost Estimation Process
1. **Determine your baseline fuel economy**:  
   - Reset your trip computer after a full tank.  
   - Drive normally for 2–3 tanks.  
   - Note the average MPG or L/100 km.

2. **Plan your route**:  
   - Use Google Maps or similar to get the total distance.  
   - Note elevation changes or heavy traffic areas.

3. **Adjust for conditions**:  
   - Add 10–20% to fuel consumption for highway speeds, hills, or AC use.  
   - Add 20–30% for city driving with frequent stops.

4. **Research fuel prices**:  
   - Use apps like GasBuddy (US) or PetrolPrices (UK) to find average prices along your route.  
   - Account for regional price differences (e.g., urban vs. rural, state/province taxes).

5. **Calculate total cost**:  
   - Apply the formulas above.  
   - Include a 5–10% buffer for unexpected detours or price fluctuations.

## Pro Tips & Best Practices for Reducing Fuel Costs
- **Drive smoothly**: Maintain steady speeds and anticipate traffic to avoid hard braking.
- **Observe speed limits**: Reducing highway speed from 75 mph to 65 mph can improve MPG by 10–15%.
- **Use cruise control**: On highways, it maintains constant speed and saves fuel.
- **Reduce weight**: Remove unnecessary items from your trunk or roof rack.
- **Maintain your vehicle**: Keep tires inflated to the recommended PSI and follow service schedules.
- **Plan fuel stops**: Fill up in areas with historically lower prices (e.g., near refineries or in competitive markets).
- **Consider alternative routes**: A slightly longer highway route may be cheaper than a shorter city route.

## Practical Applications
- **Trip budgeting**: Include fuel as a line item in your travel budget.
- **Vehicle comparison**: Calculate annual fuel costs when choosing between cars.
- **Fleet management**: Optimize routes and driver training to reduce operational costs.
- **Commuting analysis**: Compare the cost of driving vs. public transport or carpooling.
- **Environmental impact**: Estimate CO₂ emissions using fuel consumption (1 gallon ≈ 8.9 kg CO₂).

## Practice Calculating Fuel Costs

### Scenario 1: Basic Road Trip
You’re driving **400 miles** in a car that averages **32 MPG**. Fuel costs **£1.75/gallon**.  
**Task**:  
1. Calculate fuel needed: `400 ÷ 32 = 12.5 gal`  
2. Calculate total cost: `12.5 × £1.75 = **£21.88**`

### Scenario 2: European Tour
You’re renting a car in France for a **550 km** trip. Its efficiency is **5.2 L/100 km**. Fuel costs **€1.90/litre**.  
**Task**:  
1. Fuel needed: `(550 × 5.2) ÷ 100 = 28.6 L`  
2. Total cost: `28.6 × €1.90 = **€54.34**`

### Scenario 3: Commuting Costs
Your daily round-trip commute is **38 miles**. Your car gets **25 MPG** in mixed driving. Fuel is **£1.80/gallon**.  
**Task**:  
1. Weekly fuel: `(38 × 5) ÷ 25 = 7.6 gal`  
2. Weekly cost: `7.6 × £1.80 = **£13.68**`  
3. Annual cost (50 weeks): **£684**

### Scenario 4: Vehicle Comparison
You’re choosing between two cars for a **300-mile** trip:  
- **Car A**: 28 MPG  
- **Car B**: 22 MPG  
Fuel: **£2.00/gallon**  
**Task**:  
1. Car A cost: `(300 ÷ 28) × £2.00 = **£21.43**`  
2. Car B cost: `(300 ÷ 22) × £2.00 = **£27.27**`  
3. Savings with Car A: **£5.84** per trip

### Scenario 5: Speed Impact
Your car gets **35 MPG at 60 mph** but only **30 MPG at 75 mph**. For a **100-mile** trip at **£2.05/gallon**:  
**Task**:  
1. Cost at 60 mph: `(100 ÷ 35) × £2.05 = **£5.86**`  
2. Cost at 75 mph: `(100 ÷ 30) × £2.05 = **£6.83**`  
3. Extra cost for speeding: **£0.97** (and you save only 20 minutes)

### How accurate are fuel cost calculators?
They’re highly accurate if you input **real-world fuel economy** and **current fuel prices**. Manufacturer MPG ratings are often 10–20% higher than actual performance, so use your own observed data for best results.

### What’s the difference between MPG and L/100 km?
- **MPG (Miles per Gallon)**: Measures distance per unit of fuel. **Higher = more efficient**.  
- **L/100 km (Litres per 100 km)**: Measures fuel per unit of distance. **Lower = more efficient**.  
They are inversely related: `L/100 km = 235.215 ÷ MPG` (US gallons).

### Can I use this for diesel vehicles?
Yes. The calculation is identical—just use the current **diesel price**, which may differ from gasoline.

### Why is my actual fuel cost different from the estimate?
Common reasons:  
- Aggressive driving or heavy traffic  
- Using air conditioning or heating  
- Carrying extra weight or using roof racks  
- Driving at high speeds  
- Poor vehicle maintenance (e.g., low tire pressure)  
- Regional fuel price variations

### How can I reduce my fuel costs?
- Drive at moderate speeds (55–65 mph on highways)  
- Accelerate and brake smoothly  
- Keep tires properly inflated  
- Remove unnecessary weight  
- Use cruise control on highways  
- Plan routes to avoid traffic and hills  
- Compare fuel prices and fill up in cheaper areas

### Does air conditioning affect fuel economy?
Yes. Using AC can reduce fuel economy by **5–25%**, depending on the vehicle and conditions. At low speeds, opening windows is more efficient; at highway speeds, AC is better (windows increase drag).

### How do I calculate my actual MPG?
1. Fill your tank completely and reset the trip odometer.  
2. Drive normally until the next fill-up.  
3. Note the miles driven and gallons added.  
4. Calculate: `MPG = Miles Driven ÷ Gallons Added`

### Can I estimate CO₂ emissions from fuel use?
Yes. Burning 1 gallon of gasoline produces **~8.9 kg of CO₂**. For diesel, it’s **~10.2 kg/gallon**. Multiply your fuel consumption by these factors to estimate your carbon footprint.

### What is the most fuel-efficient speed?
For most cars, **45–55 mph (72–88 km/h)** is the sweet spot. Efficiency drops sharply above 60 mph due to aerodynamic drag, which increases with the **square of speed**.

### How do electric vehicles compare?
EVs don’t use fuel, but you can calculate **electricity cost**:  
`Cost = (kWh/100 km × Distance ÷ 100) × Electricity Rate (£/kWh)`  
For example, a 15 kWh/100 km EV driving 400 km at £0.28/kWh costs **£16.80**—often cheaper than gasoline.
