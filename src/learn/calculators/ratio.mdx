## What Is a Ratio?
A ratio compares two or more quantities of the same kind. It can be written in three common forms:
- **Colon notation**: `3:5`
- **Word form**: "3 to 5"
- **Fraction form**: `3/5` (though note: a ratio is a comparison, not necessarily a part-to-whole like a fraction)

Ratios can involve **two terms** (e.g., `2:3`) or **more** (e.g., `1:2:5` for cement:sand:gravel in concrete).

### Simplifying Ratios: Finding the Simplest Form
Just like fractions, ratios can be simplified by dividing all terms by their **greatest common divisor (GCD)**.

**Steps to Simplify**:
1.  List all numbers in the ratio.
2.  Find the GCD of all numbers.
3.  Divide each term by the GCD.

*Example*: Simplify `18:24`  
- GCD of 18 and 24 is **6**  
- `18 ÷ 6 = 3`, `24 ÷ 6 = 4`  
- Simplified ratio: **`3:4`**

For **three or more terms**, the process is the same:  
`12:18:30` → GCD = 6 → `2:3:5`

### Scaling Ratios: Enlarging or Reducing Proportionally
Ratios maintain their relationship when all terms are multiplied or divided by the same number.

- **To scale up**: Multiply all terms by a factor  
  *Example*: Double `2:3` → `4:6`
- **To scale down**: Divide all terms by a factor  
  *Example*: Halve `10:15` → `5:7.5` (or multiply by 2 to eliminate decimal: `10:15`)

This is essential in **recipes**, **maps**, and **model building**.

### Solving Proportions: Finding the Missing Value
A **proportion** states that two ratios are equal: `a:b = c:d`

This can be solved using **cross-multiplication**:
> `a × d = b × c`

*Example*: If `3:4 = x:20`, find `x`  
- `3 × 20 = 4 × x` → `60 = 4x` → `x = 15`

### Converting Ratios to Other Forms
- **Ratio to Fraction**: For `a:b`, the fraction of the first part is `a/(a+b)`  
  *Example*: In a `2:3` mix, the first component is `2/(2+3) = 2/5` of the total.
- **Ratio to Percentage**: Convert the fraction to a percentage  
  *Example*: `2/5 = 0.4 = 40%`

## Pro Tips & Common Mistakes
- **Always use the same units**: Convert `2 hours : 30 minutes` to `120 min : 30 min` before simplifying.
- **Don’t confuse ratio with fraction**: `3:5` is not the same as `3/5` in meaning (though mathematically related).
- **Simplify before scaling**: It’s easier to work with `1:2` than `15:30`.
- **Use ratios for consistency**: In baking, a `5:3` flour-to-water ratio ensures consistent dough regardless of batch size.

## Worked Examples & Practice Problems

### 1. Simplify These Ratios
1. `25:40` → GCD=5 → **`5:8`**
2. `1.5:2.5` → Multiply by 2 → `3:5`
3. `12:18:24` → GCD=6 → **`2:3:4`**

### 2. Recipe Scaling
A cake recipe uses a **flour:sugar:butter** ratio of `4:2:1`.  
You have **200g of butter**. How much flour and sugar?

- Butter = 1 part = 200g  
- Flour = 4 parts = **800g**  
- Sugar = 2 parts = **400g**

### 3. Map Scale
A map has a scale of **1:50,000**.  
If two towns are **8 cm apart on the map**, how far are they in real life?

- `1 cm : 50,000 cm`  
- `8 cm : 400,000 cm = 4,000 m = **4 km**`

### 4. Solve the Proportion
`5:7 = x:49`  
- `5 × 49 = 7 × x` → `245 = 7x` → **`x = 35`**

### 5. Ratio to Percentage
In a class, the **boys:girls** ratio is `3:5`.  
What percentage of the class is boys?

- Total parts = 3 + 5 = 8  
- Boys = `3/8 = 0.375 = **37.5%**`

2. A paint mix uses **red:blue:white = 2:3:5**. How much blue is needed for 500ml total?
3. If `x:12 = 9:4`, find `x`.

### What is the difference between a ratio and a proportion?
A **ratio** compares quantities (e.g., `2:3`). A **proportion** states that two ratios are equal (e.g., `2:3 = 4:6`). Ratios are the building blocks; proportions are equations involving ratios.

### How do I simplify a ratio with decimals?
Multiply all terms by 10, 100, etc., to eliminate decimals, then simplify.  
*Example*: `0.4:1.2` → ×10 → `4:12` → **`1:3`**

### Can ratios have more than two numbers?
Yes! Many real-world ratios involve three or more parts, like concrete (`1:2:4` = cement:sand:aggregate) or cocktail recipes.

### How do I check if two ratios are equivalent?
Cross-multiply. If `a:b` and `c:d` are equivalent, then `a × d = b × c`.  
*Example*: `3:4` and `9:12` → `3×12=36`, `4×9=36` → **equivalent**.

### Why do we simplify ratios?
Simplified ratios are easier to understand, compare, and scale. `1:2` is clearer than `50:100`.

### How are ratios used in business?
Common business ratios include:
- **Profit margin** = Profit : Revenue
- **Debt-to-equity** = Total Debt : Shareholder Equity
- **Current ratio** = Current Assets : Current Liabilities

### Can I add or subtract ratios?
Not directly. Ratios describe relationships, not absolute values. You must convert to actual quantities first.

### What if the ratio involves different units?
Convert to the same unit first.  
*Example*: `2 kg : 500 g` → `2000 g : 500 g` → **`4:1`**
